Human Rights October 10, 2026

UNESCO Human Rights Weekly Report 2026-10-10

Overview

During the period of 3–10 October 2026, UNESCO published a range of human rights activities. A joint UNESCO–GANHRI issue brief on digital-age rights was launched at the Human Rights Council, while separate events addressed online hate speech, the right to access information, and education rights for women and girls. A notable finding from the International Day for Universal Access to Information event in Freetown was that only 33% of information access requests were fully answered by governments, down from 42% the previous year. UNESCO also awarded its 2026 Prize for Girls' and Women's Education to projects in Morocco and Peru.

How to Strengthen Human Rights in the Digital Age?

Date: 6 October 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/how-strengthen-human-rights-digital-age

UNESCO and the Global Alliance of National Human Rights Institutions (GANHRI) launched a joint issue brief at a side event during the 63rd session of the Human Rights Council, showing how National Human Rights Institutions can monitor, report on, and act on rights issues both online and offline. The brief combines two frameworks: the ROAM-X Indicators, which assess whether a country's Internet environment is rights-based and inclusive, and guidance for assessing how digital platforms affect human rights. Together, they are designed to help these institutions advise policymakers, handle complaints, and contribute to Universal Periodic Review reporting.

When Hate Becomes the Norm: Language, Violence and Accountability in the Digital Environment

Date: 6 October 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/when-hate-becomes-norm-language-violence-and-accountability-digital-environment

UNESCO's Almaty office and the 'Aikyn Sanarip' Coalition held an interactive session in Osh, Kyrgyzstan, on 25 September 2026, as part of Yntymak Week 2026. Approximately 90 participants — including activists, bloggers, journalists, and local officials — examined how online hate speech, cyberbullying, and toxic communication affect the safety, social cohesion, and participation of young people and women. The session explored the balance between free expression and countering harmful content, and highlighted the importance of building media and information literacy.

Right to Information Takes Center Stage in Freetown

Date: 9 October 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/right-information-takes-center-stage-freetown

Sierra Leone hosted the 2026 global celebration of the International Day for Universal Access to Information in Freetown from 28–30 September 2026, alongside the 17th International Conference of Information Commissioners, drawing around 200 attendees from 41 countries. The event focused on information integrity in the digital age, and a second Global Right to Information Stress Test found that requesters received everything they asked for in only 33% of cases, down from 42% the previous year. The celebration also marked the 25th anniversary of UNESCO's Information for All Programme and the establishment of Sierra Leone's national committee for the programme.

UNESCO Awards 2026 Prize for Girls' and Women's Education to Transformative Projects in Morocco and Peru

Date: 9 October 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-awards-2026-prize-girls-and-womens-education-transformative-projects-morocco-and-peru

UNESCO's 2026 Prize for Girls' and Women's Education was awarded to Project Soar in Morocco, which helps underserved adolescent girls stay in school through local facilitators, community action, and menstrual health support, and to CARE Peru's Girls with Opportunities project, which helps rural and Indigenous girls complete secondary school. Each laureate received US$50,000. The awards were presented at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris ahead of the International Day of the Girl.
