Human Rights June 20, 2026

UNESCO Human Rights Weekly Report 2026-06-20

Overview

The week of June 13–20, 2026 saw UNESCO take several significant actions related to human rights, particularly in the areas of press freedom and safety of journalists, protection of cultural heritage, and physical safety and rights in sport. UNESCO's Director-General issued condemnations of journalist killings in Colombia, Myanmar, and Sudan, reflecting the organization's ongoing commitment to press freedom as a fundamental human right. A strike on the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra World Heritage site on June 15 prompted a UNESCO statement condemning attacks on protected cultural property under international humanitarian law. On June 15, UNESCO also launched new policy recommendations on violence-free sport at the CIGEPS session, and the 11th session of the General Assembly of States Parties to the Intangible Cultural Heritage Convention convened at UNESCO Headquarters on June 17–18.

UNESCO Condemns Strike on Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra World Heritage Site

Date: June 15, 2026
Summary: UNESCO issued a statement condemning a reported strike on June 15 that hit the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra, a UNESCO World Heritage property and one of Ukraine's most significant spiritual and cultural landmarks. The strike reportedly caused significant damage to the exterior and interior of the Dormition Cathedral, with adjacent historic structures also impacted. UNESCO stressed that attacks on cultural property protected under international humanitarian law deprive communities of access to culture, education, and shared spaces essential for recovery and social cohesion. As of June 2026, UNESCO has verified damage to 536 cultural sites across Ukraine since the start of the war.
Source: UNESCO statement on Ukraine | UNESCO

UNESCO Director-General Condemns Killing of Journalist Cristian Herrera in Colombia

Date: June 15, 2026
Summary: UNESCO's Director-General condemned the killing of journalist Cristian Hernando Herrera Nariño in Cúcuta, Colombia. Herrera served as editor-in-chief of two digital news outlets — Cúcuta Real and Cúcuta al Rojo Vivo — and was known for his reporting on organized crime, drug trafficking, smuggling, corruption, and armed violence. He was reportedly shot and killed by an unidentified assailant in the presence of his family. UNESCO called on Colombian authorities to conduct a full investigation and ensure accountability, reaffirming that journalist safety is a cornerstone of human rights and democracy.
Source: UNESCO Director-General condemns the killing of journalist Cristian Herrera in Colombia | UNESCO

UNESCO Director-General Condemns Killings of Journalists in Myanmar and Sudan

Date: June 13–15, 2026
Summary: UNESCO's Director-General issued condemnations of journalist killings in multiple conflict-affected countries. Win Htut Oo and Htet Myat Thu, journalists in Myanmar, were killed while carrying out their journalistic work, prompting UNESCO to call for an immediate and thorough investigation. Separately, UNESCO condemned the killing of Halima Idris Salim, a journalist in Sudan. These statements reflect UNESCO's ongoing monitoring through its Observatory of Killed Journalists and the organization's commitment to press freedom and the safety of media workers as human rights imperatives.
Source: UNESCO Director-General condemns killings of journalists Win Htut Oo and Htet Myat Thu in Myanmar | UNESCO

UNESCO Launches Policy Recommendations: Toward Safe Sport

Date: June 15, 2026
Summary: During the Ordinary Session of UNESCO's Intergovernmental Committee for Physical Education and Sport (CIGEPS), UNESCO and the Sport & Rights Alliance (SRA) launched the report "Toward Safe Sport: Policy Recommendations from People Impacted by Violence in Sport." Informed by the lived experiences of athletes who have faced abuse, harassment, and violence, the report provides concrete policy recommendations for governments, sports bodies, and international organizations. It reflects UNESCO's broader commitment to protecting individual rights and dignity, recognizing sport as an important arena for advancing human rights.
Source: Toward Safe Sport: UNESCO launches policy recommendations from people impacted by violence | UNESCO

11th Session of the General Assembly of the Intangible Cultural Heritage Convention

Date: June 17–18, 2026
Summary: The 11th session of the General Assembly of the States Parties to the Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage convened at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris. Intangible cultural heritage — encompassing oral traditions, performing arts, social practices, rituals, and traditional craftsmanship — is recognized as a fundamental aspect of human identity and cultural rights. The General Assembly reviewed the implementation of the Convention, discussed governance matters, and engaged with communities whose cultural rights are protected under this international framework.
Source: Eleventh session of the General Assembly - UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage
