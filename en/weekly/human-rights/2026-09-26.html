Human Rights September 26, 2026

UNESCO Human Rights Weekly Report 2026-09-26

Overview

The week of September 19–26, 2026 saw a limited number of dedicated human rights announcements from UNESCO, though two notable developments were confirmed. On September 25, UNESCO published the outcomes of a youth peacebuilding leadership program held in Lima, Peru, covering topics including Indigenous rights, gender equality, and violence prevention. Also on September 25, Pope Leo XIV made an official visit to UNESCO headquarters, delivering remarks on human dignity, AI governance, youth welfare, and interreligious dialogue. An International Day for Universal Access to Information event is scheduled for September 28–30 in Sierra Leone (outside the target week), reflecting UNESCO's ongoing promotion of information access as a fundamental human right.

UNESCO Strengthens Youth Leadership to Build Peaceful, Violence-Free Societies

Date: September 25, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-strengthens-youth-leadership-build-peaceful-violence-free-societies

Nearly 40 young Peruvians aged 18–35 gathered in Lima from September 21–23, 2026, for the Youth-driven Communities for Peace (YoD-CoP) initiative, led by UNESCO's Mahatma Gandhi Institute of Education for Peace and Sustainable Development (MGIEP) in collaboration with UNESCO Peru and the Pontifical Catholic University of Peru. Over three days, participants engaged in global citizenship education, socioemotional learning, and gender approaches to develop actionable projects addressing inequality and violence. Youth projects focused on environmental protection, Indigenous rights, gender equality, and violence prevention, responding to Peru's high rates of youth unemployment and gender-based violence affecting over one-third of young women.

Pope Leo XIV Visits UNESCO — Addresses Human Dignity in the Age of AI

Date: September 25, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/pope-leoxiv-unesco

Pope Leo XIV made an official visit to UNESCO headquarters in Paris on September 25, 2026, marking the first papal visit in over 40 years since Pope John Paul II in 1980. He addressed the international community on artificial intelligence, youth, peace, and interreligious dialogue, with a strong emphasis on protecting vulnerable populations in the digital age. Key themes included the right of young people to be safeguarded from cyberbullying and algorithmic manipulation, the preservation of religious heritage, and shared commitments between the Holy See and UNESCO to uphold human dignity and accountability in technological advancement.

For Reference: International Day for Universal Access to Information (Outside Target Week)

Date: September 28–30, 2026 (outside the target reporting week; upcoming)
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/days/universal-access-information

UNESCO promotes universal access to information as a fundamental human right and an essential enabler of the Sustainable Development Goals. The 2026 international celebration is scheduled for September 28–30 in Sierra Leone. Access to information is closely linked to freedom of expression, democratic accountability, and the right to education.
