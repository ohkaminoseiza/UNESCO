Human Rights October 24, 2026

UNESCO Human Rights Weekly Report 2026-10-24

Overview

During the week of October 17–24, 2026, UNESCO published two human rights-related reports: a peace-building initiative between journalists and security forces in South Sudan (October 22) and a survey on the online threats facing Nepal's digital content creators with implications for freedom of expression (October 19). The organization also updated its announcement of the 5th UNESCO Global Forum against Racism and Discrimination (The Hague, October 29–30), originally published October 13, which falls outside this week's target range.

UNESCO Is Helping to Build Trust between Journalists and Security Personnel in South Sudan

Date: October 22, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-helping-build-trust-between-journalists-and-security-personnel-south-sudan

UNESCO held a two-day dialogue workshop in Wau, Western Bahr el Ghazal State, South Sudan, bringing together more than 30 police officers, soldiers, national security personnel, and journalists to build mutual understanding. Sessions covered press freedom, journalist safety, and shared legal frameworks, and included practical simulated scenarios such as crime scenes and public gatherings. This initiative is funded by the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA) and is running in seven locations across South Sudan. The country ranked 118 out of 180 in Reporters Without Borders' 2026 World Press Freedom Index—nine places lower than in 2025—and the effort is especially timely ahead of the country's first elections, scheduled for December 2026. Women journalists face additional risks including online and offline sexual and gender-based violence.

What Nepal's Digital Creators Face Online: New Survey Provides Insights

Date: October 19, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/what-nepals-digital-creators-face-online-new-survey-provides-insights

UNESCO and UNFPA held an event in Nepal focused on building a safer, more inclusive digital environment, presenting UNESCO's survey report on content creators and UNFPA's review of laws and policies on technology-facilitated gender-based violence. Among the survey's findings: 36.7% of respondents took no action when they encountered misinformation; 26.6% had experienced hate speech; and 25.0% had faced trolling or doxxing. Nepal's Police Cyber Bureau registered nearly 19,000 cybercrime cases in fiscal year 2024/25. Speakers emphasized that safety must be designed into digital platforms from the outset, and that young, rural, and Indigenous-language creators must have a voice in shaping digital policy—framing freedom of expression online as inseparable from personal safety.

5th UNESCO Global Forum against Racism and Discrimination (The Hague, October 29–30)

Date: Originally published October 13, 2026 (outside target week); last updated October 21, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/fifth-unesco-global-forum-against-racism-and-discrimination-take-place-hague

(Note: The original publication date of October 13 falls outside this week's scope; included here because the article was updated October 21 and the event takes place October 29–30.)

UNESCO, co-hosted by the Kingdom of the Netherlands, will hold the 5th Global Forum against Racism and Discrimination at the World Forum in The Hague on October 29–30, 2026. Under the theme "From Commitment to Accountability: Change the System, Change the Future," the Forum will bring together ministers, mayors, academics, civil society, youth, and media professionals. High-profile speakers include Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten, UNESCO Deputy Director-General Äsa Réger, scholars Ibram X. Kendi and Kimberlé Crenshaw, actress Viola Davis, and UN Special Rapporteur on racism Ashwini K.P. Discussions will cover structural racism, racial profiling, algorithmic bias, online hate speech, and the further development of the UNESCO Reparatory Justice Toolkit. Key sessions will be livestreamed.
