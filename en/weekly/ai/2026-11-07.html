AI November 7, 2026

UNESCO AI Weekly Report 2026-11-07

Overview

During the week of October 31 to November 7, 2026, UNESCO published four official articles on AI ethics and governance. Key topics include progress on data governance for ethical AI in Latin America, a new partnership with Google to support content creators in Brazil, the expansion of UNESCO's global network of AI supervisory authorities, and practical AI governance support for countries in Africa and Small Island Developing States. All initiatives are anchored in UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of AI, adopted unanimously by all 193 Member States in 2021.

Latin America Makes Progress in Data Governance for Ethical and Inclusive Artificial Intelligence

Date: 6 November 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/latin-america-makes-progress-data-governance-ethical-and-inclusive-artificial-intelligence

UNESCO released a Spanish-language version of its Data Governance Toolkit: Navigating the Data in the Digital Age (originally launched in July 2025 by the Broadband Commission for Sustainable Development with UNDP, ITU, and the African Union Commission), expanding its reach across Latin America and the Caribbean. In Peru, the Toolkit has informed a National AI Strategy 2026–2030 and a National Data Governance Strategy 2026–2030, alongside the 2025 approval of regulations for AI Law No. 31814. UNESCO and UNDP's joint training course "Data Governance for Inclusive Digital & AI Futures" drew over 1,000 applications across its first two 2026 cohorts, with participants from 22 countries — 84% from government or regulatory bodies. An Indigenous Language Data Commons Incubator call received more than 180 submissions from over 50 countries, with selection currently underway.

UNESCO and Google Partner to Empower Brazilian Content Creators

Date: 2 November 2026 (last updated 5 November 2026)
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-and-google-partner-empower-brazilian-content-creators

UNESCO and Google jointly developed the Super Creators Toolkit, a practical guide for using AI ethically, grounded in media and information literacy (MIL). A pilot workshop in São Paulo on 1 October 2026 brought together 22 digital content creators from across Brazil with a combined audience of 93 million followers on major social media platforms, to test the draft toolkit. Participants discussed how they use AI for research, scripting, and editing, and examined risks such as inaccurate outputs and embedded bias. The Toolkit is built around the SIFT method and is set to launch formally later in 2026, with YouTube and Redes Cordiais as additional partners.

Global Network on AI Supervision Led by UNESCO Pushes for Practical Cooperation

Date: 2 November 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/global-network-ai-supervision-led-unesco-pushes-practical-cooperation

The Global Network of AI Supervisory Authorities (GNAIS), led by UNESCO, convened at the 4th UNESCO Global Forum on the Ethics of AI in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia (14–17 September 2026). The Network has grown to 42 AI supervisory authorities, with Saudi Arabia's SDAIA and Luxembourg's CNPD serving as co-chairs alongside UNESCO. Members conducted an AI audit workshop using the Eticas AI Risk Taxonomy — featuring a case study on an AI-based hiring tool — and held a panel on international cooperation with representatives from Cuba, Luxembourg, Saudi Arabia, and Thailand. Key priorities confirmed at the meeting include information sharing, capacity building, and greater harmonization of regulatory approaches; supervisory authorities wishing to join can contact gnais@unesco.org.

UNESCO AI Ethics Action Cohorts Advance Practical AI Governance

Date: 2 November 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-ai-ethics-action-cohorts-advance-practical-ai-governance

UNESCO's AI Ethics Action Cohorts brought together 17 public-sector participants from 16 countries in Africa and Small Island Developing States (SIDS) to translate findings from UNESCO's Readiness Assessment Methodology (RAM) into concrete national AI governance projects. At the 4th Global Forum on the Ethics of AI in Riyadh, participants received practical training in the AI for Environment and Ecosystems Toolkit for Policymakers and the Ethical Impact Assessment (EIA) tool. The initiative is funded through Japan's Funds-in-Trust (JFIT) under the project "Supporting countries in building effective AI Governance through implementing the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI." Participants from Suriname and Curaçao shared how they plan to apply the tools in their home countries.
