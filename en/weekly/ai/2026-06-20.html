AI June 20, 2026

UNESCO AI Weekly Report 2026-06-20

Overview

During the week of June 13–20, 2026, UNESCO continued to advance its artificial intelligence agenda with a focus on AI in education and AI governance. Key highlights include the publication of a new article on the future of AI and education (June 16), the recognition of four initiatives promoting responsible AI use in education through the King Hamad Bin Isa Al-Khalifa Prize for ICT in Education, and continued preparation for the Global Dialogue on AI Governance scheduled for Geneva on July 6–7, 2026. UNESCO also reinforced its ethics-driven AI ecosystem through ongoing policy frameworks and capacity-building initiatives worldwide.

AI and Education: Towards a Human-Centred Future of Digital Learning

Date: June 16, 2026
Summary: UNESCO published a new article examining the implications of artificial intelligence for the future of education, titled "AI and Education: towards a human-centred future of digital learning." The publication explores how AI can transform learning environments and teaching practices while keeping human development, equity, and rights at the centre. It reaffirms UNESCO's guiding principles that AI in education must promote inclusion, protect learners' rights, and support teachers rather than replace them. The article draws on UNESCO's global work on AI competency frameworks and its Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research.
Source: Artificial intelligence in education - AI | UNESCO

UNESCO Recognizes Four Initiatives Promoting Responsible AI in Education

Date: June 2026 (this week)
Summary: UNESCO awarded the King Hamad Bin Isa Al-Khalifa Prize for the Use of Information and Communication Technologies (ICTs) in Education to four initiatives from Belgium, Brazil, Egypt, and the United Kingdom, under the theme "Preparing learners and teachers for the ethical and responsible use of artificial intelligence." Marking its 20th anniversary, the prize recognized: AI4InclusiveEducation (Belgium), offering AI civic education for disadvantaged youth aged 10–18; Piauí Inteligência Artificial (Brazil), integrating AI as a compulsory subject in public school curricula; Experience AI (UK/Raspberry Pi Foundation/Google DeepMind), reaching over 1.2 million young people in 24 countries; and an initiative from Egypt. The award ceremony was held at the University of Bahrain.
Source: UNESCO recognizes four initiatives promoting the responsible use of AI in education | UNESCO

Preparations Advance for Global Dialogue on AI Governance, Geneva (July 6–7)

Date: June 2026 (this week)
Summary: With under three weeks remaining before the Global Dialogue on AI Governance in Geneva (July 6–7, 2026), UNESCO and the ITU—as joint UN Secretariat for the initiative—are urging early registration, recommended by June 25. The Dialogue will be held alongside the World Summit on the Information Society (WSIS) Forum 2026 (July 6–10) and ITU's AI for Good Global Summit (July 7–10). It aims to ensure that AI governance frameworks reflect the priorities of all nations, not just the most technologically advanced, and will contribute to shaping multilateral AI policy.
Source: Global Dialogue on AI Governance, Geneva, 6–7 July | UNESCO
