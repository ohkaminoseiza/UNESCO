AI September 26, 2026

UNESCO AI Weekly Report 2026-09-26

Overview

During the week of September 19–26, 2026, UNESCO published several significant updates on AI ethics and education. The organization launched the third edition of its free online course on digital citizenship and AI, now newly available in Portuguese. A call for papers on AI ethics research was opened, and coverage of Caribbean participation in the Global Forum on AI Ethics was published. On September 25, Pope Leo XIV made an official visit to UNESCO headquarters, delivering remarks on artificial intelligence, youth, peace, and interreligious dialogue — the first papal visit to UNESCO in over 40 years.

UNESCO Opens Third Edition of Its Course on Digital Citizenship and Artificial Intelligence, Now Also Available in Portuguese

Date: September 25, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-opens-third-edition-its-course-digital-citizenship-and-artificial-intelligence-now-also

UNESCO launched the third cohort of its free online MOOC on digital citizenship and artificial intelligence, beginning October 12, 2026. The course is now available in English, Spanish, and — for the first time — Portuguese. To date, more than 33,000 people from over 113 countries have registered, with a 34% retention rate. The curriculum covers five thematic modules including algorithms, disinformation, digital rights, and ethical AI use, with a certificate available to participants who complete at least 80% of the coursework.

Caribbean Voices at the 4th UNESCO Global Forum on the Ethics of AI

Date: September 23, 2026 (last updated September 24, 2026)
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/caribbean-voices-4th-unesco-global-forum-ethics-ai

The 4th UNESCO Global Forum on the Ethics of AI (GFEAI 2026), held September 14–17 in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, featured significant participation from Caribbean nations including The Bahamas, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Suriname, and Trinidad and Tobago. Representatives highlighted the underrepresentation of Caribbean data in AI systems and the importance of maintaining human-centered education. The forum underscored that building inclusive AI governance for Small Island Developing States requires dedicated capacity-building spaces and peer-learning opportunities, with UNESCO committing to ensure the region's perspectives shape global AI governance discussions.

Call for Papers: Advancing Research on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence

Date: September 23, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/call-papers-advancing-research-ethics-artificial-intelligence

UNESCO and the AI and Society Institute at the École normale supérieure in Paris have opened a call for research proposals on AI ethics, targeting recent PhD graduates (within five years of completion). The submission deadline is October 30, 2026, and selected authors will present their work at an academic event in Paris on December 10, 2026. Research may subsequently be published in the Tribune of the Conseil de l'intelligence artificielle et du numérique, aiming to foster interdisciplinary exchange in line with UNESCO's 2021 Recommendation on the Ethics of AI.

Pope Leo XIV Visits UNESCO — Addresses AI, Youth, and Human Dignity

Date: September 25, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/pope-leoxiv-unesco

Pope Leo XIV made an official visit to UNESCO headquarters in Paris on September 25, 2026, marking the first papal visit in over 40 years since Pope John Paul II's address in 1980. He delivered remarks before the international community on artificial intelligence, youth welfare, peace, and interreligious dialogue. Key themes included protecting young people from cyberbullying and algorithmic manipulation, the importance of UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of AI, and the need for accountability and respect for human dignity in the age of digital technology.

For Reference: 4th Global Forum on AI Ethics Concludes (Outside Target Week)

Date: September 18, 2026 (outside the target reporting week)
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/global-cooperation-ethical-ai-governance-reinforced-unescos-4th-global-forum-ethics-ai-saudi-arabia

The GFEAI 2026 in Riyadh, which drew over 6,300 participants from 50+ Member States, marked five years of implementation of UNESCO's 2021 AI Ethics Recommendation. New tools launched at the forum included an environmental impact toolkit for policymakers, an enhanced RAM 2.0 assessment methodology, and a meta-analysis synthesizing insights from 55 country reports. The forum reinforced international cooperation toward inclusive and sustainable AI governance in areas including gender equality, education, health, and environmental impact.
