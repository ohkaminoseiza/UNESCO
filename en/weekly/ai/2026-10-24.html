AI October 24, 2026

UNESCO AI Weekly Report 2026-10-24

Overview

During the week of October 17–24, 2026, UNESCO advanced multiple AI-related initiatives worldwide. Key activities included advancing Indigenous-led AI through data commons, analyzing AI's systemic impact on education, launching Sri Lanka's national AI readiness assessment using UNESCO's updated RAM 2.0, selecting 50 young leaders to foster inclusive dialogue in the AI era, and hosting a webinar on open licensing and knowledge commons. These actions collectively reflect UNESCO's sustained commitment to ethical, inclusive, and rights-based AI governance.

Turning Global Interest into Support for Indigenous-led AI

Date: October 21, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/turning-global-interest-support-indigenous-led-ai

On September 22, 2026, on the sidelines of the 81st UN General Assembly in New York, UNESCO, Microsoft, and The GovLab hosted a Foundation Briefing Luncheon on advancing public-interest AI through data commons for Indigenous languages and cultures. Participants, including Indigenous leaders, international organizations, civil society, funders, researchers, and industry representatives, discussed how Indigenous communities can gain meaningful control over how their data, languages, and cultural knowledge are used in AI systems—going beyond inclusion to letting communities set priorities and govern benefits. UNESCO's Guilherme Canela De Souza Godoi stressed that public-interest AI depends on "who benefits, who participates, and whose rights and knowledge are respected." The New Commons Incubator for Indigenous Languages and Cultures was highlighted as a framework for community-led data governance.

UNESCO's Digital Transformation Collaborative: Charting How AI Is Reshaping Education Systems

Date: October 19, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unescos-digital-transformation-collaborative-charting-how-ai-reshaping-education-systems

UNESCO's Digital Transformation Collaborative (DTC), a technology subgroup of the Global Education Coalition, held a Strategy Lab at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris on September 11, 2026, with more than 200 participants. The Lab applied its Six Pillars Framework—covering Content and solutions, Capacity and culture, Connectivity and infrastructure, Cost and sustainability, Data and evidence, and Coordination and leadership—to assess how AI is changing education systems. Key findings emphasized that access to AI is no longer sufficient; quality, teacher training for ethical AI use, data transparency, and equitable governance of AI systems are central concerns. Discussions will continue at the Global Education Coalition annual meeting in March 2027.

Sri Lanka Launches UNESCO's AI Readiness Assessment Methodology (RAM)

Date: October 22, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/sri-lanka-launches-unescos-ai-readiness-assessment-methodology-ram

UNESCO's Regional Office for South Asia and Sri Lanka's Ministry of Digital Economy formally launched UNESCO's AI Readiness Assessment Methodology (RAM 2.0), making Sri Lanka one of the first countries to apply the updated version. A multi-stakeholder National Steering Committee—including government, private sector, academia, civil society, and UNDP—is guiding the assessment across five cities: Colombo, Jaffna, Kandy, Batticaloa, and Galle. The process will produce recommendations to feed into Sri Lanka's national AI strategy and digital transformation agenda. RAM is one of UNESCO's key tools for implementing its Recommendation on the Ethics of AI, and as of August 2026 has been deployed in more than 75 Member States, with 60 assessments complete.

50 Emerging Leaders Selected to Foster Dialogue and Inclusion in the Age of AI

Date: October 21, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/50-emerging-leaders-selected-foster-dialogue-and-inclusion-age-ai

UNESCO selected 50 young leaders from 38 countries (52% women) for the second edition of the Youth for Peace: UNESCO Intercultural Leadership Programme, chosen by an international jury of 26 members from 6,786 applications. This cohort focuses specifically on how artificial intelligence affects social cohesion—examining its opportunities and risks for inclusive societies. Each participant receives a USD 10,000 grant for a community intercultural dialogue project, alongside mentoring, specialized training, and masterclasses. The programme opens officially on October 26 and culminates in a Global Youth Dialogue for Peace in China.

Open Licensing and the Knowledge Commons in the Age of AI (Webinar)

Date: October 20, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/open-licensing-and-knowledge-commons-age-artificial-intelligence

The OER Dynamic Coalition hosted an interactive virtual webinar examining whether AI will expand or restrict the open knowledge commons. Participants explored how open licensing decisions affect who benefits from AI-generated and AI-assisted knowledge—and how open licensing can ensure that AI's benefits remain equitable and accessible. The audience included policymakers, educators, researchers, information professionals, civil society, and technology developers.
