Environment September 26, 2026

UNESCO Environment Weekly Report 2026-09-26

Overview

During the week of September 19–26, 2026, UNESCO's ocean science body, the Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission (IOC), played a central role in advancing ocean and climate commitments at the 81st UN General Assembly (UNGA81). On September 24, the EU and Canada co-launched the OceanEye International Alliance for the Global Ocean Observing System (GOOS), mobilizing over CAD $339 million in commitments from 31 nations to strengthen ocean observation infrastructure. A high-level UN General Assembly meeting on sea level rise was also held on September 24, aimed at adopting a UN Declaration on Sea-Level Rise that had been in preparation since early 2026. These initiatives underscore the growing urgency of sustained international investment in ocean monitoring as a foundation for climate action.

Governments at UN General Assembly Support Global Ocean Observation

Date: September 24, 2026
Source: https://www.ioc.unesco.org/en/articles/governments-un-general-assembly-support-global-ocean-observation

The European Union and Canada co-launched the OceanEye International Alliance for the Global Ocean Observing System (GOOS) at the 81st UN General Assembly, securing over CAD $339 million in commitments from 31 member nations. The EU pledged €92 million and Canada committed CAD $82 million over five years to strengthen ocean observation infrastructure critical for climate forecasting, maritime safety, and coastal protection. The Alliance aims to address critical data gaps — such as the absence of near-equatorial monitoring buoys in the western Pacific — and ensure sustainable, long-term funding for global ocean observation networks. Canadian Prime Minister stated: "The ocean we share cannot be understood, or protected, by any nation alone."

IOC Brings Ocean Science to the Forefront of UNGA81 — Including Sea Level Rise Declaration

Date: September 16, 2026 (overview of UNGA81 activities including September 24 events)
Source: https://www.ioc.unesco.org/en/articles/ocean-unga81

UNESCO's Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission (IOC) advanced ocean science as a core pillar of international accountability across three key UNGA81 events. On September 18, the IOC spotlighted SDG 14 (Life Below Water) at the SDG Moment, reporting on ocean acidification monitoring and national investment tracking. On September 24, a High-Level Meeting on Sea Level Rise convened at the UN General Assembly, with IOC providing scientific evidence on sea level trends and the role of its Global Sea Level Observing System (GLOSS), which manages over 2,000 tide gauge stations worldwide. The IOC emphasized that "understanding a changing ocean starts with the science and data to measure it" and advocated for sustained investment in science and capacity, particularly for Small Island Developing States.
