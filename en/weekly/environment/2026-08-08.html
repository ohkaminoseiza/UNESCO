Environment August 8, 2026

UNESCO Environment Weekly Report 2026-08-08

Overview

During the week of August 1–8, 2026, UNESCO advanced its environmental agenda through several significant initiatives. UNESCO's Intergovernmental Hydrological Programme (IHP) opened its 2026 call for new ecohydrology demonstration sites worldwide, accepting applications through August 31. A national workshop in Ethiopia promoted climate-smart, inclusive education for pastoralist communities facing the intensifying impacts of climate change. Kenya also renewed its commitment to sustainable rangeland management ahead of major international events in Mongolia. Together, these activities reflect UNESCO's continued leadership in connecting science, education, and policy to address the world's most pressing environmental challenges.

Call for New Ecohydrology Demonstration Sites 2026

Date: 3 August 2026

UNESCO's Intergovernmental Hydrological Programme (IHP) launched its 2026 global call for applications for new ecohydrology demonstration sites in all countries, with submissions due by 31 August 2026. These sites function as living laboratories that implement nature-based solutions to address interconnected water and environmental challenges at the catchment scale, contributing valuable knowledge on how ecosystems can be managed sustainably. The global network currently encompasses 66 demonstration sites in 39 countries, providing participating sites with greater international visibility, peer recognition, and opportunities for cross-border knowledge exchange.

Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/call-new-ecohydrology-demonstration-sites-2026

Advancing Climate-Smart Education for Pastoralist Communities

Date: 7 August 2026

UNESCO's International Institute for Educational Planning (IIEP-UNESCO) and Ethiopia's Ministry of Education convened a two-day national workshop in Addis Ababa (28–29 July 2026) under the Climate Smart Education Systems Initiative (CSESI) to advance climate-resilient and inclusive education for pastoralist communities. The workshop validated research findings and co-developed strategic guidance for building flexible, gender-responsive, and culturally appropriate education systems that meet the needs of mobile and semi-nomadic populations. With increasing temperatures, prolonged droughts, and erratic rainfall disrupting children's access to schooling and undermining traditional livelihoods, the recommendations generated will support evidence-based education planning and stronger cross-sector collaboration to ensure no pastoralist child is left behind.

Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/advance-climate-smart-education-pastoralist-communities

The Road to Mongolia: Kenya Renews Commitment to Sustainable Rangeland Management and Pastoral Development

Date: August 2026 (International Year of Rangelands and Pastoralists)

In preparation for UNCCD COP17 in Mongolia and the International Year of Rangelands and Pastoralists (IYRP 2026), Kenya renewed its commitment to sustainable rangeland management and pastoral development through the adoption of a joint communiqué with key international partners. The communiqué calls for concerted action to protect the ecosystems on which millions of pastoral communities worldwide depend, advancing sustainable land use practices amid mounting climate pressures. This commitment aligns with UNESCO's broader conservation and sustainable development goals, including the protection of biodiversity and the support of communities that are among the most vulnerable to environmental degradation.

Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/road-mongolia-kenya-renews-commitment-sustainable-rangeland-management-and-pastoral-development
