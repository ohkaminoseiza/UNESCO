Environment October 24, 2026

UNESCO Environment Weekly Report 2026-10-24

Overview

During the week of October 17–24, 2026, UNESCO published several significant environment-related updates. The UN Biodiversity Conference (COP17) opened in Yerevan, Armenia (October 19–30), marking the first global review of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, with UNESCO showcasing its network of over 2,300 designated sites as concrete conservation solutions. Other highlights include the launch of environmental DNA (eDNA) sampling in the remote Crozet Archipelago, Central Indian Ocean countries charting their ocean science priorities through 2037, and UNESCO equipping more than 530 cultural heritage professionals from 46 countries with El Niño preparedness resources.

UNESCO Showcases Solutions for Biodiversity at COP17 in Armenia

Date: October 21, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-showcases-solutions-biodiversity-cop17-armenia

The 17th Conference of the Parties to the Convention on Biological Diversity (COP17), themed "Taking action for nature," opened in Yerevan, Armenia, on October 19, 2026—the first global review of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework adopted in 2022. UNESCO highlighted that its network of over 2,300 designated sites, including 797 Biosphere Reserves, maintains stable wildlife populations against a backdrop of global biodiversity decline; approximately 900 million people live within these sites. A joint UNESCO assessment warns that, without further action, more than one in four designated sites could reach critical tipping points by 2050. UNESCO is co-convening the Nature and Culture Summit and Science Forum (October 24–25) and Education Day (October 27) at COP17, and the Guardians of the Biosphere exhibition is on display in Yerevan's Charles Aznavour Square.

Tracing Marine Life from a Water Sample: eDNA Sampling Begins in the Crozet Archipelago

Date: October 23, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/tracing-marine-life-water-sample-edna-sampling-begins-crozet-archipelago

UNESCO's citizen science initiative eDNA Expeditions has launched its second phase, beginning environmental DNA sampling in the Crozet Archipelago. The first phase (2022) covered four UNESCO Marine World Heritage sites in the French Southern and Antarctic Lands and detected 181 species and 418 taxa. The second phase (2026) covers seven sites—including the original four—enabling time-series tracking of biodiversity changes. The non-invasive, low-cost eDNA method is especially well suited to remote sites like the Crozet Archipelago that receive only about three ship visits per year, and the initial sampling coincided with the ongoing COP17 in Yerevan.

IOCINDIO-II: Central Indian Ocean Countries Chart Their Ocean Science Priorities to 2037

Date: October 19, 2026
Source: https://www.ioc.unesco.org/en/articles/iocindio-ii-central-indian-ocean-countries-chart-their-ocean-science-priorities

The IOC-UNESCO Sub-Commission for the Central Indian Ocean (IOCINDIO) held its second intergovernmental session in Bali, Indonesia, October 12–14, 2026. Member States set priority activities for 2028–2029 to inform UNESCO's next programme and budget, and defined strategic goals for the IOC Medium-Term Strategy for 2030–2037. A new Secretariat was established at INCOIS in Hyderabad, India, and Seychelles and Kenya joined as the 14th and 15th members respectively. The session also addressed implementation of the BBNJ Agreement (in force since January 17, 2026), tsunami preparedness, and expanding ocean data and biodiversity infrastructure in the region.

UNESCO Equips over 500 Culture Professionals with El Niño Preparedness Resources

Date: October 21, 2026
Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-equips-over-500-culture-professionals-el-nino-preparedness-resources

UNESCO held regional webinars October 2–5, 2026, for cultural heritage professionals and national authorities across Latin America and the Caribbean, Africa, and Asia-Pacific. More than 530 participants from 46 countries attended (roughly two-thirds women, including representatives from 11 Small Island Developing States). UNESCO introduced the Culture x El Niño Readiness Check—a ten-step, four-phase self-assessment tool that produces a readiness score and identifies priority gaps. The Heritage Emergency Fund is already supporting preparedness work in Ecuador, Peru, and Timor-Leste. UNESCO Director for Culture and Emergencies Krista Pikkat summarized the rationale: "uncertainty is not a reason to wait. It is a reason to prepare."
