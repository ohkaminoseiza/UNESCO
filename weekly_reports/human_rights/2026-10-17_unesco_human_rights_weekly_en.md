# UNESCO Human Rights Weekly Report 2026-10-17

## Overview

During the period of 10–17 October 2026, UNESCO published significant updates on press freedom, journalist safety, and girls' education. On 14 October, UNESCO's Director-General condemned the killing of an Indigenous community journalist in Guatemala. On 16 October, UNESCO held a press briefing on its biennial report on the safety of journalists, revealing that global progress on ending impunity for journalist killings has reversed. Two articles published immediately before the target week — the 2026 GEM Gender Report (8 October) and the UNESCO Prize for Girls' and Women's Education ceremony (9 October) — are included below because they are directly tied to the International Day of the Girl Child (11 October) and contain significant human rights content (published outside target week).

---

## UNESCO Director-General Condemns the Killing of Journalist Evelio Lisandro López Sánchez in Guatemala

**Date:** 14 October 2026
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-director-general-condemns-killing-journalist-evelio-lisandro-lopez-sanchez-guatemala

UNESCO Director-General Khaled El-Anany condemned the killing of community journalist Evelio Lisandro López Sánchez, who was shot and killed by unknown assailants in San Pedro Necta, Huehuetenango, Guatemala on 2 October 2026. He founded the digital news portal Huehue es Noticias and was a member of the Maya Mam Indigenous People. He had previously reported threats to his life because of his reporting. The statement calls on Guatemalan authorities to conduct a thorough investigation and bring those responsible to justice.

## Online Press Briefing: UNESCO Report Reveals Most Journalists' Killings Are Not Brought to Justice

**Date:** 16 October 2026
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/online-press-briefing-unesco-report-reveals-most-journalists-killings-are-not-brought-justice-and

UNESCO held an online press briefing on its biennial Director-General's Report on the Safety of Journalists, covering the 2024–2025 period. The report finds that global progress in ending impunity for the killing of journalists has gone into reverse. The spike in impunity is attributed in part to a surge in journalist killings in conflict zones, and the report also examines new technological weapons as threats to journalists. According to UNESCO, nine out of ten journalist killings remain unsolved. The briefing featured Mariya Gabriel, Assistant Director-General for Communication and Information, and Sylvie Coudray, Director of Freedom of Expression and Media Development.

## UNESCO Awards 2026 Prize for Girls' and Women's Education to Transformative Projects in Morocco and Peru

**Date:** 9 October 2026 (published outside target week)
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-awards-2026-prize-girls-and-womens-education-transformative-projects-morocco-and-peru

Ahead of the International Day of the Girl Child on 11 October, UNESCO awarded the 2026 UNESCO Prize for Girls' and Women's Education to two laureates at its Paris headquarters on 9 October. Each received US$50,000. "Project Soar in a Bag" (Morocco) supports adolescent girls in staying in school through trained local facilitators, community action, and menstrual health support. CARE Peru's "Girls with Opportunities (GWO)" project helps rural and Indigenous girls complete secondary school and plan for further study or work.

## 2026 GEM Gender Report: Gender Gaps in Education Narrow Globally, Yet Early Marriage Remains a Barrier

**Date:** 8 October 2026 (published outside target week)
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/gender-gaps-education-narrow-globally-yet-early-marriage-remains-major-barrier-girls-education-new

Published ahead of the International Day of the Girl Child (11 October), the 2026 UNESCO Global Education Monitoring (GEM) Gender Report finds that gender gaps in education have narrowed substantially worldwide, with girls now enrolled at roughly the same rates as boys from pre-primary through secondary levels. However, deep inequalities persist in rural areas and poor households. The share of girls married before age 18 fell globally from 25% in 2000 to 18% in 2025, yet remained at 31% in sub-Saharan Africa. Around six million pregnant or young mothers aged 10–19 were out of school in sub-Saharan Africa in 2024, and fewer than 5% in the region return to school. Only 35% of countries explicitly protect the right to education for pregnant and parenting girls. The report calls for ending child marriage, preventing early pregnancy, and guaranteeing young mothers' right to continue their education.
