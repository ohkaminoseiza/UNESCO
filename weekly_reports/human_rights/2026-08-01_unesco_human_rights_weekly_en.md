# UNESCO Human Rights Weekly Report 2026-08-01

## Overview

During the week of July 25–August 1, 2026, UNESCO focused its human rights activities on press freedom, journalist safety, and safe learning environments. The Director-General issued condemnations of journalist killings in Yemen and Mexico, reaffirming UNESCO's mandate to defend freedom of expression worldwide. UNESCO also presented its violence prevention and positive school climate initiatives at a joint event with the Municipality of Santiago, Chile. Separately, the World Heritage Committee's designation of endangered sites in conflict zones underscored the human rights dimensions of cultural heritage protection.

---

## UNESCO Director-General Condemns Killing of Journalist Mohammad Aydah in Yemen

**Date:** July 29, 2026

UNESCO's Director-General issued a strong condemnation of the killing of journalist Mohammad Aydah in Yemen, calling on Yemeni authorities to conduct a thorough investigation and bring those responsible to justice. Yemen remains one of the world's most dangerous countries for journalists, where ongoing armed conflict severely limits press freedom and access to information. UNESCO's condemnation reaffirms the organization's mandate as the lead UN agency for the safety of journalists and the fight against impunity for crimes against media workers.

**Source:** [https://www.unesco.org/en/safety-journalists](https://www.unesco.org/en/safety-journalists)

---

## UNESCO Director-General Condemns Killing of Journalist Alejandro Leyva Aguilar in Mexico

**Date:** July 28, 2026

The UNESCO Director-General condemned the killing of journalist Alejandro Leyva Aguilar in Mexico and urged the Mexican authorities to investigate the murder swiftly and ensure accountability. Mexico is one of the most dangerous countries for journalists in Latin America, and this condemnation reflects UNESCO's ongoing efforts to monitor journalist safety and pressure governments to protect media workers. UNESCO continues to provide capacity-building support to member states through the International Programme for the Development of Communication (IPDC) to strengthen the safety of journalists at the national level.

**Source:** [https://www.unesco.org/en/safety-journalists](https://www.unesco.org/en/safety-journalists)

---

## UNESCO Presents Work on Positive School Climates and Violence Prevention in Santiago

**Date:** July 31, 2026

At an event co-organized with the Municipality of Santiago, Chile, UNESCO presented its work on fostering positive school climates and preventing violence in and through education. The event was held under UNESCO's "Safe to Learn" initiative, which addresses various forms of school violence including bullying, corporal punishment, and gender-based violence, all of which undermine students' right to safe and quality education. UNESCO called on policymakers, educators, and communities to adopt evidence-based approaches that create inclusive, respectful, and non-violent learning environments for all learners.

**Source:** [https://www.unesco.org/en/health-education/safe-learning-environments](https://www.unesco.org/en/health-education/safe-learning-environments)

## Six New Sites Join UNESCO's List of World Heritage in Danger: Conflict and Climate Threats

**Date:** July 26, 2026

The UNESCO World Heritage Committee, meeting at its 48th session in Busan, inscribed six sites on the List of World Heritage in Danger, underscoring the human rights dimensions of cultural heritage under threat. Three sites were inscribed via emergency procedure — the Boma-Badingilo Migratory Landscape (South Sudan), Mount Amel Castles (Lebanon), and Sebastia (State of Palestine) — reflecting the severe impact of armed conflict on heritage and communities. Three previously inscribed sites were also added to the Danger List, including the Ancient City of Tauric Chersonese and its Chora (Ukraine), where the temporary occupation by the Russian Federation prevents the State Party from ensuring the site's protection. UNESCO emphasized that the destruction of cultural heritage in conflict zones constitutes a human rights violation and called on the international community to stand in solidarity for protection.

**Source:** [https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/facing-major-threats-six-new-sites-join-unescos-list-world-heritage-danger-three-them-emergency](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/facing-major-threats-six-new-sites-join-unescos-list-world-heritage-danger-three-them-emergency)
