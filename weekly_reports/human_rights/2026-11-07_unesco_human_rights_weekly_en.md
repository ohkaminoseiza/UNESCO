# UNESCO Human Rights Weekly Report 2026-11-07

## Overview

During the week of October 31 to November 7, 2026, UNESCO released two significant publications related to press freedom and journalist safety as expressions of human rights. The Director-General's biennial Report on the Safety of Journalists and the Danger of Impunity documented a rise in the global impunity rate for journalist killings to 86% in 2024–2025, reversing a decade-long downward trend. A second article reported on UNESCO's global initiative to counter strategic lawsuits against public participation (SLAPPs) used to silence journalists. Both items reflect UNESCO's core position that press freedom is a fundamental pillar of human rights.

## Six in Seven Journalist Killings Go Unpunished, UNESCO Reports

**Date:** 2 November 2026  
**Source:** https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/11/1168719 (UN News coverage of the UNESCO report)

UNESCO's biennial Director-General's Report on the Safety of Journalists and the Danger of Impunity found that 189 journalists were killed during 2024–2025, a 20% increase compared to the previous two-year period. The global impunity rate rose to 86% — up from 85% — marking the first increase in over a decade after a gradual decline since 2012. Of the 189 killed, 127 (67%) died in conflict zones; drones were flagged for the first time as a new threat to journalists in conflict settings. In non-conflict countries, however, the impunity rate fell from 94% in 2012 to 77%, and the total number of journalists killed in non-conflict countries (85 in 2024–2025) reached its lowest level in 18 years.

**Note:** UNESCO's own website (unesco.org) was returning errors at the time of this report. Information was confirmed through UN News and multiple independent news sources.

## UNESCO Continues to Address Abusive Lawsuits Silencing Journalists

**Date:** 5 November 2026  
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-continues-address-abusive-lawsuits-silencing-journalists

UNESCO published an update on its Global Initiative on Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation (SLAPPs) Against Freedom of Expression. A global needs assessment conducted jointly with the Bonavero Institute of Human Rights at the University of Oxford found that 78% of respondents reported an increase in the use of SLAPPs to target journalists over the past five years. Such abusive litigation is designed to financially and psychologically burden journalists and newsrooms, creating a chilling effect on reporting. UNESCO supports legislation against SLAPPs and provides training for judicial officials in affected countries.
