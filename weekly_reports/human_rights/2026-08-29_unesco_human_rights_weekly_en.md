# UNESCO Human Rights Weekly Report 2026-08-29

## Overview

During the period of August 22–29, 2026, UNESCO published three items with direct relevance to human rights. Key activities included a roundtable in India on advancing safe and inclusive sport for girls (August 27), a commemorative event in Mexico's Chiapas region marking the 60th International Literacy Day (August 28), and the publication of the final evaluation of UNESCO's Strategy for Youth and Adult Literacy 2020–2025 (August 24). The week reflected UNESCO's ongoing work across multiple human rights dimensions: the right to education, gender equality, and the rights of marginalized groups.

## UNESCO Convenes Roundtable on Advancing Safe and Inclusive Sport for Girls in India

- **Date:** August 27, 2026
- **URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-convenes-roundtable-advancing-safe-and-inclusive-sport-girls-india
- UNESCO organized a roundtable in India focused on advancing safe and inclusive sporting environments for girls. The event addresses gender discrimination in sport, intersecting with UNESCO's human rights mandate and the principle of non-discrimination. This initiative builds on UNESCO's Roadmap against Racism and Discrimination in sport and its broader commitment to gender equality and the rights of women and girls.

## UNESCO Celebrates 60th International Literacy Day in Chiapas, Mexico

- **Date:** August 28, 2026
- **URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-celebrates-60th-international-literacy-day-chiapas-mexico
- UNESCO organized a commemorative event in Mexico's Chiapas region ahead of the official International Literacy Day (September 8), marking 60 years of the observance. Literacy is recognized in UNESCO's framework as an integral component of the right to education — a fundamental human right. The event highlighted ongoing efforts to reach marginalized populations who remain excluded from literacy programs.

## Final Evaluation of the UNESCO Strategy for Youth and Adult Literacy (2020–2025)

- **Date:** August 24, 2026
- **URL:** https://www.uil.unesco.org (UNESCO Institute for Lifelong Learning)
- UNESCO published the final evaluation of its five-year Strategy for Youth and Adult Literacy (2020–2025), assessing outcomes across member states. The report frames literacy as both a human right and a driver of sustainable development, and identifies persistent challenges in reaching out-of-school youth and adults. Findings will inform the development of a successor strategy framework.

## Reference: Notable Content from the Preceding Period (August 14–21, 2026)

### Five Years On: Keeping Learning Alive for Girls and Women in Afghanistan

- **Date:** August 14, 2026 (updated August 19, 2026)
- **URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/five-years-keeping-learning-alive-girls-and-women-afghanistan
- UNESCO reported that its community-based literacy and skills training programs in Afghanistan have reached over 69,000 learners — girls and women barred from secondary and higher education since 2021. The article frames the work explicitly around "restoration of their right to education." An estimated 2.4 million girls remain excluded from secondary education.

### Monitoring Education Rights Worldwide: Results of the Eleventh Consultation on the 1960 Convention against Discrimination in Education

- **Date:** August 18, 2026
- **URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/monitoring-education-rights-worldwide-results-eleventh-consultation-member-states-1960-convention
- UNESCO published findings from its eleventh member-state consultation on implementation of the 1960 Convention against Discrimination in Education — a core human rights instrument. The report reveals momentum toward inclusive education policies but flags persistent inequalities and barriers for marginalized groups. Member States were called to submit updated reports by November 15, 2026.

### Towards a Barrier-Free Education: Advances and Challenges in Inclusive Education (SIRIED 2024)

- **Date:** August 19, 2026
- **URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/hacia-una-educacion-sin-barreras-avances-y-desafios-en-la-educacion-inclusiva-que-evidencian-los-0
- A UNESCO regional report on inclusive education in Latin America and the Caribbean, drawing on SIRIED 2024 data. Key findings: one in five students with disabilities attends special-education schools, and over a third drop out before completing upper secondary education. The report is framed explicitly around Article 24 of the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.
