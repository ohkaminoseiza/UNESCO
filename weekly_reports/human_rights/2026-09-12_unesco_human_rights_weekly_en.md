# UNESCO Human Rights Weekly Report 2026-09-12

## Overview

During the week of September 5–12, 2026, UNESCO made several significant announcements and held events related to human rights. On September 8, UNESCO published a alarming report showing that attacks on schools surged by 33% in 2025, calling for stronger international protection of the right to education. The International Literacy Day 2026 celebrations in Chiapas, Mexico (September 8–9) highlighted literacy as a fundamental human right under the theme "Literacy for people, the planet and prosperity." At Digital Learning Week on September 9, over 25 education ministers adopted a joint ministerial statement reaffirming education as a human right in the age of AI. On September 10, UNESCO Goodwill Ambassadors gathered in Athens to advance UNESCO's mission on peace and human dignity. These events reflect UNESCO's sustained commitment to protecting human rights across education, literacy, digital equity, and peace-building domains.

---

## As Attacks on Schools Rise by 33% in 2025, UNESCO Calls for Stronger Protection of Education

**Date:** September 8, 2026

**Summary:**
UNESCO reported a dramatic increase in attacks on educational institutions, with 1,680 verified incidents in 2025 — a 33% rise compared to the previous year. The organization highlighted that approximately 258 million children globally face education disruption due to crises, with 93 million entirely out of school. UNESCO urged the international community to strengthen compliance with international humanitarian law to ensure the protection of schools, students, and teachers in conflict-affected areas.

**Source:** [https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/attacks-schools-rise-33-2025-unesco-calls-stronger-protection-education](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/attacks-schools-rise-33-2025-unesco-calls-stronger-protection-education)

## International Literacy Day 2026: UNESCO Celebrates "Literacy for People, the Planet and Prosperity" in Mexico

**Date:** September 8–9, 2026

**Summary:**
UNESCO, in collaboration with the Government of Mexico, hosted the International Literacy Day 2026 global celebration in Chiapas, Mexico, with the opening ceremony held in the indigenous community of San Juan Chamula. The event, themed "Literacy for people, the planet and prosperity," brought together government officials, educators, civil society organizations, and learners from around the world. Six laureates of the UNESCO International Literacy Prizes 2026 were announced, recognizing outstanding literacy programmes worldwide.

**Source:** [https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/international-literacy-day-global-celebration-and-literacy-prizes-2026-award-ceremony](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/international-literacy-day-global-celebration-and-literacy-prizes-2026-award-ceremony)

## International Day to Protect Education from Attack: UNESCO Launches "Crossing" Installation

**Date:** September 9, 2026

**Summary:**
On the occasion of the International Day to Protect Education from Attack, UNESCO launched the "Crossing" installation at the Piazza area of its Headquarters in Paris. The installation, open to the public from September 9 to 14, is designed to raise awareness and inspire action for the protection of education and support for students, teachers, and education communities in conflict zones. The event underscores UNESCO's commitment to safeguarding the right to education in situations of armed conflict.

**Source:** [https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/commemoration-international-day-protect-education-attack-launch-crossing-installation](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/commemoration-international-day-protect-education-attack-launch-crossing-installation)

## Education Ministers Affirm Education as a Human Right and Common Good in the Age of AI

**Date:** September 9, 2026

**Summary:**
Over 25 education ministers adopted a joint ministerial statement at UNESCO's Digital Learning Week 2026, calling for education to be "sustained as a human right and a common good in the age of artificial intelligence." The statement outlines eight priorities for AI-era education policy, including protecting teacher agency, ensuring public oversight of AI systems in education, and safeguarding learners through age-appropriate AI measures. A UNESCO survey accompanying the statement found that 93% of academics believe students use generative AI for assignments, underscoring the urgency of clear institutional guidelines.

**Source:** [https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/education-ministers-call-education-remain-common-good-age-ai-unescos-digital-learning-week](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/education-ministers-call-education-remain-common-good-age-ai-unescos-digital-learning-week)

## UNESCO Goodwill Ambassadors Gather in Athens to Advance Peace and Human Dignity

**Date:** September 10, 2026

**Summary:**
UNESCO's distinguished Goodwill Ambassadors convened in Athens for their first gathering outside UNESCO Headquarters, bringing together prominent advocates to address global challenges facing human rights and peace. The three-day event featured speakers including Amal Clooney and focused on how artificial intelligence impacts cultural preservation and human rights. Participants emphasized that AI development must remain guided by ethical principles and serve the public interest, advancing UNESCO's core mission of building peace through education, science, culture, and communication.

**Source:** [https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-goodwill-ambassadors-gather-athens-exchange-people-and-peace-changing-world](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-goodwill-ambassadors-gather-athens-exchange-people-and-peace-changing-world)
