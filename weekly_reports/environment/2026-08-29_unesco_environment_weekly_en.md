# UNESCO Environment Weekly Report 2026-08-29

## Overview

During the period of August 22–29, 2026, UNESCO carried out two significant environment-related activities, both tied to the UN Convention to Combat Desertification's 17th Conference of the Parties (UNCCD COP17), which ran August 17–28 in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia under the theme "Restoring Land. Restoring Hope." UNESCO's contributions included a high-level dialogue on integrated water-land-people approaches (August 25) and a dedicated side event on knowledge, culture, land, and water for resilient restoration (August 26). COP17 concluded on August 28 with the announcement of a $1.3 billion finance portfolio for land restoration and drought resilience. UNESCO actively contributed science, Indigenous knowledge, and cultural heritage perspectives throughout the conference.

## UNESCO High-level Dialogue on Water-Land-People: Connecting Science, Policy and Practice

- **Date:** August 25, 2026
- **URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-high-level-dialogue-water-land-people-connecting-science-policy-and-practice
- UNESCO, in collaboration with the UNCCD Secretariat, World Bank, and The Nature Conservancy (TNC), convened a high-level dialogue at UNCCD COP17 in Ulaanbaatar to examine integrated approaches to water, ecosystems, and land for strengthening resilience, protecting biodiversity, and supporting livelihoods. Government ministers and senior representatives of international organizations discussed moving beyond fragmented sectoral approaches toward coordinated action across water, land, climate, and biodiversity agendas. The dialogue showcased practical examples from UNESCO-designated sites (Biosphere Reserves, World Heritage sites, Geoparks) and underscored the critical role of Indigenous Peoples and local communities in sustainable land-water solutions.

## UNESCO for People and Planet: Knowledge, Culture, Land and Water for Resilient Restoration (COP17 Side Event)

- **Date:** August 26, 2026, 13:00–14:30 (Ulaanbaatar time)
- **URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/cop17
- UNESCO hosted a dedicated side event at UNCCD COP17 in Ulaanbaatar's Blue Zone (National Garden Park), focused on the intersection of knowledge systems, culture, land, and water in advancing resilient ecosystem restoration. The event positioned UNESCO's interdisciplinary mandate — spanning science, education, culture, and communication — as an integrated approach to tackling desertification and land degradation. It formed part of UNESCO's week-long program at COP17 (August 17–28).

## Reference: Related Content from the Preceding Period (August 3–21, 2026)

### UNESCO's Work with Pastoralists Highlights Lessons for COP17

- **Date:** August 5, 2026
- **URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unescos-work-pastoralists-highlights-lessons-cop17
- UNESCO demonstrated how pastoralist communities' centuries-old knowledge of sustainable land management can address desertification, drought, and climate change. The organization works directly with pastoralists across more than 200 designated sites including World Heritage properties and Biosphere Reserves, documenting thousands of environmental indicators from communities across West, Horn, and East Africa. This work continued into the COP17 period.

### The Road to Mongolia: Kenya Renews Commitment to Sustainable Rangeland Management and Pastoral Development

- **Date:** August 3, 2026
- **URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/road-mongolia-kenya-renews-commitment-sustainable-rangeland-management-and-pastoral-development
- UNESCO joined Kenya's National Conference on the International Year of Rangelands and Pastoralists, where government officials and community stakeholders adopted nine commitments to protect and restore rangelands. Kenya pledged to strengthen policy frameworks, expand biosphere reserves, and ensure inclusive participation from women, youth, and marginalized communities in rangeland governance, contributing to COP17 outcomes.
