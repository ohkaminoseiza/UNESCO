# UNESCO Environment Weekly Report 2026-07-25

## Overview

The week of July 18–25, 2026 was a particularly active period for UNESCO's environmental activities. The centrepiece event is the 48th Session of the UNESCO World Heritage Committee, which opened on July 19 in Busan, Republic of Korea, and runs through July 29. The committee is examining nominations for 30 new World Heritage sites and reviewing the state of conservation of 147 inscribed sites, with 53 currently on the List of World Heritage in Danger due to climate change, armed conflict, and overdevelopment. Also this week, the outcomes of UNESCO's landmark first Global Conference of the International Decade of Sciences for Sustainable Development (IDSSD), held July 15–17 in Paris, were published. Additionally, on July 24 UNESCO released an article highlighting how Pacific Island nations are weaving Indigenous knowledge into climate education.

---

## 48th Session of the UNESCO World Heritage Committee Opens in Busan

**Date:** 19–29 July 2026 (ongoing)  
**URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/world-heritage/committee-2026

The 48th Session of the UNESCO World Heritage Committee opened on July 19, 2026, in Busan, Republic of Korea — the first time the Republic of Korea has hosted the event. The session will examine nominations for 30 new sites for inscription on the World Heritage List, along with proposals related to three already listed sites, and will review the state of conservation of 147 properties. From July 21 to 24, the Committee reviewed priority measures to ensure site preservation; deliberations on new inscriptions are scheduled to begin July 24. Currently, 53 properties appear on UNESCO's List of World Heritage in Danger, threatened by armed conflict, climate change, and overdevelopment. The session is chaired by H.E. Mr Lee Byong Hyun (Republic of Korea).

## UNESCO IDSSD Global Conference Outcomes Published

**Date:** Week of 18–25 July 2026 (conference held 15–17 July 2026)  
**URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-convenes-scientific-community-global-conference-science-sustainable-development

The outcomes of UNESCO's first Global Conference of the International Decade of Sciences for Sustainable Development (IDSSD 2024–2033) were made public this week following the hybrid event held July 15–17 at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris. Under the theme "Science in Action: Charting a Sustainable and Equitable Future for All," the conference brought together ministers, UN leaders, leading scientists, and representatives from government, industry, civil society, and Indigenous knowledge communities. Key outcomes include the launch of the first IDSSD Global Report (2024–2026), which assesses progress in mobilizing science for sustainable development. Parallel thematic sessions covered climate change and resilience, biodiversity and nature-based solutions, water security, and harnessing AI for sustainable development. Implementation of UNESCO's Recommendation on Open Science was reported by 81 governments.

## Pacific Indigenous Practices Shape Climate Education for Future Generations

**Date:** 24 July 2026  
**URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/how-pacific-practices-are-shaping-climate-education-future-generations

UNESCO published an article on July 24 highlighting how Pacific Island countries are integrating Indigenous knowledge and cultural practices into climate education through the Climate-Smart Education Systems Initiative (CSESI). The article features the Indigenous Fijian concept of "curui" — the practice of repairing woven mats or sails to strengthen them while preserving their original purpose — as a metaphor for how education systems can be built on existing cultural knowledge to help learners navigate a changing climate. The article also emphasizes linkages with the Pacific Employability, Transferable and All-Life Skills (PETALS) framework, which promotes competencies grounded in Pacific values such as reciprocity, resilience, and environmental stewardship.
