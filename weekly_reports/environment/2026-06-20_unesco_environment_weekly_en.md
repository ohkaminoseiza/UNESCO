# UNESCO Environment Weekly Report 2026-06-20

## Overview

During the week of June 13–20, 2026, UNESCO's environmental activities focused primarily on the intersection of cultural and natural heritage protection and the broader climate action agenda. The most significant development was UNESCO's condemnation on June 15 of a strike on the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra World Heritage site, highlighting the ongoing threat to World Heritage sites in conflict zones. This week also followed the close of the application period for the Sultan Qaboos Prize for Environmental Conservation (deadline June 15), and continued UNESCO's work on environmental sustainability through its World Heritage programme and ongoing climate action initiatives. With the 48th session of the World Heritage Committee (Busan, Republic of Korea, July 20–29) approaching, UNESCO also published materials on the role of Biosphere Reserves and Global Geoparks as climate action drivers, and released a new online course linking environmental and human health.

---

## UNESCO Condemns Strike on Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra World Heritage Site

**Date:** June 15, 2026  
**Summary:** UNESCO issued a statement condemning a reported strike on June 15 that hit the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra, a UNESCO World Heritage property and one of Ukraine's most significant spiritual and cultural landmarks. The strike reportedly caused significant damage to the Dormition Cathedral and adjacent historic structures. UNESCO emphasized that protecting World Heritage sites from armed conflict is inseparable from protecting global environmental and cultural patrimony. As of June 10, 2026, UNESCO has verified damage to 536 cultural sites across Ukraine (including 154 religious sites and 280 buildings of historical or artistic interest), and called for full respect of international humanitarian law protecting cultural property.  
**Source:** [UNESCO statement on Ukraine | UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-statement-ukraine)

## 48th Session of the World Heritage Committee Approaching (Busan, July 20–29)

**Date:** June 2026 (upcoming)  
**Summary:** UNESCO confirmed that the 48th session of the World Heritage Committee will take place in Busan, Republic of Korea, from July 20 to 29, 2026. The Committee will examine nominations for 30 new World Heritage sites and review the state of conservation of 147 already-inscribed sites, many of which face threats from climate change, development pressures, and conflict. The session is a key moment in UNESCO's global environmental stewardship, as the Committee also decides on sites that may be added to or removed from the List of World Heritage in Danger. Documents in preparation for the session, including conservation reports, were published in June 2026.  
**Source:** [World Heritage Committee 2026 | UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/world-heritage/committee-2026)

## New UNESCO Online Course Links Environmental and Human Health

**Date:** June 2026 (this week)  
**Summary:** UNESCO released a new online course emphasizing the connections between environmental health and human health. The course addresses emerging threats including biodiversity loss, pollution, climate change, and ecosystem degradation, and their cascading effects on human wellbeing. By situating environmental protection within the framework of health and human rights, the course provides interdisciplinary training for professionals and policymakers working at the nexus of environmental science, public health, and sustainable development.  
**Source:** [New UNESCO online course stresses link between environmental and human health | UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/new-unesco-online-course-stresses-link-between-environmental-and-human-health)

---

## Biosphere Reserves and Global Geoparks as Drivers of Sustainable Development and Climate Action

**Date:** June 2026 (this week)  
**Summary:** UNESCO published an article highlighting the role of its Biosphere Reserves and Global Geoparks as key drivers of sustainable development and global climate action. These UNESCO-designated sites serve as living laboratories where communities, scientists, and governments work together to balance conservation and sustainable use. Building on the momentum of the 38th MAB International Coordinating Council session (June 3–6) and the World Environment Day biosphere reserve designations (June 5), the article emphasizes that UNESCO's designated areas collectively protect critical ecosystems, preserve biodiversity, and provide nature-based solutions to climate change.  
**Source:** [Biosphere Reserves and Global Geoparks as drivers of sustainable development and global climate action | UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/biosphere-reserves-and-global-geoparks-drivers-sustainable-development-and-global-climate-action)
