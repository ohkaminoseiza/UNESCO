# UNESCO Environment Weekly Report 2026-07-18

## Overview

The week of 11–18 July 2026 was one of the most active periods for UNESCO's environment and sustainability work this year. The centerpiece was the first Global Conference of the International Decade of Sciences for Sustainable Development (IDSSD), convened at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris on 15–17 July, bringing together nearly 400 initiatives from 79 countries. The Large Marine Ecosystems Consultation Meeting (LME25), held 7–9 July at UNESCO HQ, strengthened global ocean governance. UNESCO's Climate Risk Informed Decision Analysis (CRIDA) methodology was elevated to UNESCO's Intergovernmental Hydrological Programme (IHP) flagship initiative status, marking a major step in institutionalizing science-based climate risk assessment tools in global policy.

---

## 2026 Global Conference of the International Decade of Sciences for Sustainable Development (IDSSD)

**Date:** 15–17 July 2026

The first Global Conference of the International Decade of Sciences for Sustainable Development (IDSSD, 2024–2033) — proclaimed by the United Nations and led by UNESCO — was held as a hybrid event at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris. Under the theme "Science in action: Charting a sustainable and equitable future for all," the conference convened leaders from government, academia, industry, civil society, and Indigenous knowledge communities. Parallel thematic sessions on 16–17 July addressed Climate Change and Resilience, Biodiversity and Nature-based Solutions, Water Security for All, Harnessing AI for Sustainable Development, Open Science, and Innovating STEM Education. Two years into IDSSD implementation, the conference confirmed that the global scientific community has endorsed nearly 400 initiatives from 79 countries and mobilized USD 50 million in support of sciences for sustainable development.

**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/2026-global-conference-international-decade-sciences-sustainable-development-2024-2033

## UNESCO Convenes Scientific Community for Global Conference on Science for Sustainable Development

**Date:** 15–17 July 2026

UNESCO announced the convening of the 2026 IDSSD Global Conference as a premier global platform to take stock of progress on science for sustainable development and help shape a resilient, inclusive post-2030 future. The event spotlights how transdisciplinary research, open science, and strengthened science–policy–society interfaces can drive more equitable futures for all. UNESCO highlighted that the IDSSD framework represents a pivotal global movement to harness the full spectrum of scientific disciplines — from natural to social sciences — for accelerated achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals.

**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-convenes-scientific-community-global-conference-science-sustainable-development

## Large Marine Ecosystems Consultation Meeting (LME25) — UNESCO HQ, 7–9 July 2026

**Date:** 7–9 July 2026

The 25th annual Large Marine Ecosystems (LME) Consultation Meeting took place at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris, bringing together representatives from Regional Seas bodies, Fisheries Management Organizations, international NGOs, and partner organizations. The meeting developed the future strategic direction of the LME Programme in response to emerging global priorities, including climate change, GEF-9 (the Global Environment Facility's ninth replenishment), and the implementation of the BBNJ Agreement (Agreement on the Conservation and Sustainable Use of Marine Biological Diversity of Areas Beyond National Jurisdiction). The meeting concluded with the 2nd Data and Information Management (DIM) Workshop, where project managers and technical experts shared advances in integrated ocean data management and marine monitoring technologies.

**Source:** https://indico.un.org/event/1023404/

## UNESCO's Climate Risk Analysis Method (CRIDA) Elevated to IHP Flagship Initiative

**Date:** July 2026 (exact date unconfirmed)

Thirty-six member states of UNESCO's Intergovernmental Hydrological Programme (IHP) endorsed a draft resolution proposing that CRIDA (Climate Risk Informed Decision Analysis) be elevated to the status of IHP Flagship Initiative. CRIDA is UNESCO's science-based methodology for assessing climate-driven water risks — including floods and droughts — and integrating that analysis into policy decision-making. The endorsement significantly strengthens the institutional standing of the tool in global climate adaptation policy, reflecting growing recognition of science-based approaches as essential to resilient water and disaster risk management.

**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-method-analysing-climate-risk-gets-flagship-initiative-status

## World Environment Day 2026: A Call for Climate Action — UNESCO World Heritage Centre

**Date:** 5 June 2026 (referenced and reported during this period)

On World Environment Day 2026, celebrated under the theme of climate action, UNESCO's World Heritage Centre reaffirmed the vital role of World Heritage sites in protecting the planet's natural and cultural treasures. The Centre underscored the severe threat that climate change poses to World Heritage sites globally and called for strengthened collaboration between ecosystem conservation and cultural heritage protection. UNESCO emphasized that preserving these sites is integral to any comprehensive response to the climate and biodiversity crises.

**Source:** https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/2900
