# UNESCO Environment Weekly Report 2026-08-22

## Overview

During the week of August 15–22, 2026, the most significant UNESCO environmental event was the 17th Conference of the Parties (COP17) to the UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD), underway in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia from August 17–28. UNESCO is actively contributing to COP17 by bringing its interdisciplinary expertise in science, culture, education, and communication to address water management, land restoration, livelihoods, and pastoralism. Related content was also released highlighting UNESCO's work with pastoralists and their traditional knowledge as solutions for combating desertification. Climate change impacts on World Heritage sites remain an ongoing concern, with biodiversity loss, desertification, and climate change recognized as deeply interconnected global challenges requiring urgent coordinated action.

## UNESCO at COP17: Restoring Land, Restoring Hope

**Date:** August 2026 (COP17 session: August 17–28, 2026)  
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/cop17-desertification

The 17th Conference of the Parties (COP17) to the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD) is taking place in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, from August 17 to 28, 2026, under the theme "Restoring Land. Restoring Hope." UNESCO is participating by spotlighting water, land restoration, pastoralist livelihoods, and indigenous knowledge, bringing its distinctive interdisciplinary mandate in sciences, culture, education, and communication to support ecosystem recovery and the communities that depend on them. Desertification, biodiversity loss, and climate change are recognized as interconnected crises requiring joint international action.

## UNESCO's Work with Pastoralists Highlights Lessons for COP17

**Date:** August 2026  
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unescos-work-pastoralists-highlights-lessons-cop17

UNESCO's unique mandate encompasses protecting indigenous knowledge systems, safeguarding cultural heritage, and conserving biodiversity through natural sciences — working directly with pastoralists in World Heritage properties, Biosphere Reserves, and Geoparks where they play an active land management role. As COP17 convenes, UNESCO highlights how the traditional knowledge of indigenous and local communities plays a crucial role in combating desertification and adapting to climate change. These pastoralist knowledge systems are positioned as a core component of UNESCO's interdisciplinary approach to sustainable land management.

## Eleven Young Scientists to Advance Research in UNESCO Biosphere Reserves

**Date:** June 16, 2026  
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/eleven-young-scientists-advance-research-unesco-biosphere-reserves

UNESCO announced eleven laureates of the 2026 MAB (Man and the Biosphere) Young Scientists Awards, with five also receiving the Prince Albert I of Monaco Award. Scientists from ten countries are conducting research across biosphere reserves worldwide, studying topics ranging from mangrove restoration and sustainable tourism to climate adaptation and marine biodiversity, as part of UNESCO's efforts to advance conservation research globally.

## Ocean Biodiversity and Climate Resilience for Sustainable Ocean Planning

**Date:** Ongoing 2026  
**Source:** https://www.ioc.unesco.org/en/biodiversity-climate-resilience-sop

UNESCO's Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission (IOC) is developing a climate vulnerability assessment tool to support implementation of the IOC Strategy for Sustainable Ocean Planning and Management. The tool helps countries assess climate and biodiversity risks, prioritize adaptation strategies, and integrate considerations of climate and biodiversity into marine protected area management — bridging ocean conservation and climate resilience at a policy level.
