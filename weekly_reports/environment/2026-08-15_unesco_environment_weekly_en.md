# UNESCO Environment Weekly Report 2026-08-15

**Period covered:** August 8–15, 2026  
**Date of issue:** August 15, 2026  
**Source:** UNESCO official website (unesco.org)

---

## Overview

This week (August 8–15, 2026), UNESCO's environment portfolio centered on climate-resilient education and the continued integration of sustainability into regional capacity-building. The flagship development was the August 12 publication of a report on UNESCO's capacity-building workshop for Caribbean teachers and officials, equipping five nations' educators with practical tools to embed Education for Sustainable Development (ESD), Climate Change Education, and Disaster Risk Reduction into school systems. Simultaneously, UNESCO kept open its call for proposals for the 2027 World Conference on Education for Sustainable Development (deadline: August 31, 2026), inviting global organizations to co-design sessions on sustainability, equity, and climate action. The broader context reflects UNESCO's recognition that biodiversity loss now constitutes a systemic economic risk and that ocean health, cultural heritage, and science-based governance are all integral to the environmental agenda.

## UNESCO Empowers Caribbean Teachers and Officials to Drive Climate-Resilient and Sustainable Education

**Date:** August 12, 2026 (last updated: August 14, 2026)  
**URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-empowers-caribbean-teachers-and-officials-drive-climate-resilient-and-sustainable-education

In partnership with the University of the West Indies, UNESCO conducted a regional capacity-building workshop in Grenada from June 30 to July 2, 2026, convening 34 educators and officials from Dominica, Grenada, Guyana, St. Lucia, and St. Vincent and the Grenadines. Participants received training in "practical approaches that can be adapted to Caribbean contexts" for integrating ESD, Climate Change Education, and Disaster Risk Reduction into national school systems, including field visits, group exercises, and the development of country-specific action plans. UNESCO will consolidate the workshop outcomes into a regional guidance note, provide ongoing technical support to participating ministries, and convene a follow-up online meeting later in 2026 to allow countries to review progress against their plans.

## Call for Proposals: UNESCO World Conference on Education for Sustainable Development 2027

**Deadline:** August 31, 2026  
**URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/sustainable-development/education/2027-conference

UNESCO is accepting proposals from organizations wishing to co-organize thematic sessions at the upcoming World Conference on Education for Sustainable Development, with submissions open through August 31, 2026. The conference will explore the intersections of sustainability, educational equity, climate action, and systemic transformation, and UNESCO is inviting governments, civil society, academic institutions, and the private sector to contribute to its design and agenda.

## 2026 Global Conference on the International Decade of Sciences for Sustainable Development

**Date:** July 15–17, 2026 (preceding this week)  
**URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/2026-global-conference-international-decade-sciences-sustainable-development-2024-2033

UNESCO hosted the inaugural Global Conference of the International Decade of Sciences for Sustainable Development (IDSSD 2024–2033) on July 15–17, 2026, marking a milestone in the global movement to harness scientific disciplines for accelerated achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals. Cross-cutting themes included ecosystems, biodiversity, climate change, and the science-policy interface, with participants from governments, research institutions, and civil society.

## UNESCO World Heritage Committee 48th Session: 25 New Sites Inscribed

**Date:** July 19–29, 2026 (preceding this week)  
**URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/world-heritage/committee-2026

UNESCO's World Heritage Committee held its 48th session in Busan, Republic of Korea from July 19–29, 2026, concluding with 25 new sites inscribed on the World Heritage List. The inscription of natural and mixed heritage sites contributes directly to UNESCO's environmental mandate — World Heritage designation provides frameworks for biodiversity recovery, climate resilience, and sustainable local livelihoods, directly aligned with the 2026 World Environment Day theme of climate action.

## UNESCO's IOC Develops Climate Vulnerability Assessment for Marine Protected Areas

**URL:** https://www.ioc.unesco.org/en/biodiversity-climate-resilience-sop

UNESCO's Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission (IOC-UNESCO) is developing a global climate vulnerability assessment tool for marine protected areas, designed to help countries assess risk and integrate both climate and biodiversity considerations into marine protected area (MPA) management plans. The initiative supports the convergence of ocean science, climate adaptation policy, and biodiversity governance at the national and international levels.

## How Caribbean Education Systems Are Adapting to Climate Change

**URL:** https://www.iiep.unesco.org/en/articles/how-caribbean-education-systems-are-adapting-climate-change  
(UNESCO International Institute for Educational Planning)

UNESCO's International Institute for Educational Planning (IIEP) published an analysis of how Caribbean education systems are responding to climate change through school infrastructure hardening, curriculum reform, and teacher training. The piece emphasizes the value of regional cross-country knowledge exchange and positions education systems as key actors — not merely targets — in climate resilience strategies.
