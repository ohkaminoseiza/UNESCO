# UNESCO Environment Weekly Report 2026-06-06

## Overview

The week of May 30 – June 6, 2026 was marked by significant UNESCO activity across environment and ecology. The centrepiece event was World Environment Day (June 5), on which UNESCO designated 14 new biosphere reserves across 14 countries and hosted the 38th session of the Man and the Biosphere (MAB) International Coordinating Council in Paraguay's Itaipu Biosphere Reserve (June 3–6). Alongside these flagship events, the week encompassed: biodiversity action tied to the International Day for Biological Diversity (May 22); a Women for Bees programme update around World Bee Day; UNESCO Africa Week 2026 focusing on water and sanitation (May 19–22); accelerated Central Asian cryosphere cooperation at the Regional Ecological Summit and a follow-up coordination forum; and IOC UNESCO's alert on escalating ocean acidification threats to marine life.

---

## World Environment Day 2026: A Call for Climate Action

- Date: 2026-06-05
- Summary: On World Environment Day 2026, UNESCO's World Heritage Centre reaffirmed the critical role of World Heritage in combating climate change, noting that 43% of natural World Heritage sites now face a "high" threat level from climate change — the highest of any threat globally. The 2026 theme framed climate action as not merely reducing carbon emissions but fundamentally rethinking economic systems and restoring humanity's relationship with the climate. UNESCO highlighted that coral reefs, glaciers, and ecosystems inscribed on the World Heritage List are facing accelerating deterioration from warming, bleaching events, and extreme weather.
- Source: [World Environment Day 2026: A Call for Climate Action – UNESCO World Heritage Centre](https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/2900)

## UNESCO Designates 14 New Biosphere Reserves on World Environment Day

- Date: 2026-06-05
- Summary: UNESCO officially designated 14 new biosphere reserves on June 5, expanding the World Network of Biosphere Reserves to 797 sites across 145 countries. The new designations span Albania, Algeria, Aruba (entire island), Azerbaijan, Cameroon, Canada (Quebec City — the world's first city-wide biosphere reserve designation), Iran, Mongolia, Montenegro, Paraguay, Philippines, Portugal, Timor-Leste, and Viet Nam. Three countries — Montenegro, Timor-Leste, and Aruba — received their first-ever biosphere reserves, while territorial extensions were approved for five existing reserves in China, Italy, and Spain.
- Source: [UNESCO designates 14 new biosphere reserves on World Environment Day](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-designates-14-new-biosphere-reserves-world-environment-day)

## UNESCO Charts a New Path for People and Nature: 38th MAB International Coordinating Council

- Date: 2026-06-05
- Summary: The 38th session of the MAB International Coordinating Council (MAB-ICC) was held June 3–6 at the Itaipu Biosphere Reserve in Paraguay, bringing together over 250 participants including government representatives from 34 countries, scientists, UN agencies, and NGOs. This was the first major governance meeting under the MAB Programme's new Hangzhou Strategic Action Plan 2026–2035, with discussions focused on advancing biodiversity conservation and sustainable development across the global biosphere reserve network. The Itaipu Biosphere Reserve — hosting nearly one million hectares, 250,000 people including Indigenous communities, and one of Earth's most threatened ecosystems, the Upper Paraná Atlantic Forest — provided a symbolic and practical backdrop for the gathering.
- Source: [UNESCO charts a new path for people and nature](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-charts-new-path-people-and-nature)

## International Day for Biological Diversity 2026: Acting Locally for Global Impact

- Date: 2026-05-22
- Summary: On International Day for Biological Diversity (May 22), UNESCO World Heritage Centre Director Lazare Eloundou Assomo delivered a message underlining the theme "Acting locally for global impact," emphasizing that global biodiversity targets can only be met through concrete local action. Despite global biodiversity indicators continuing to decline, wildlife populations monitored within UNESCO-designated sites have on average remained stable, demonstrating the effectiveness of UNESCO's conservation frameworks. UNESCO's combined network of natural World Heritage sites, biosphere reserves, and geoparks covers more than 13 million square kilometres — an area comparable in size to China and India combined — forming one of the world's largest nature conservation networks.
- Source: [Message from the Director of UNESCO World Heritage Centre for International Day for Biological Diversity 2026](https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/2899)

## Protecting Biodiversity Through Bees and Women (Women for Bees Programme)

- Date: 2026-05-22
- Summary: Around World Bee Day (May 20) and the International Day for Biological Diversity, UNESCO highlighted the achievements of the Women for Bees programme, a collaboration between UNESCO's MAB Programme and Guerlain launched in 2020. The programme has trained women beekeepers across multiple countries in UNESCO biosphere reserves, providing technical skills, income opportunities, and stronger community voices for participants. With nearly 75% of global food crops dependent on pollinators and over 40% of bee species potentially threatened according to national assessments, the initiative demonstrates how women's empowerment and biodiversity conservation can be mutually reinforcing goals.
- Source: [UNESCO protects the future of biodiversity through bees and women](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-protects-future-biodiversity-through-bees-and-women)

## Africa Week 2026: Ensuring Sustainable Water and Sanitation

- Date: 2026-05-19
- Summary: UNESCO held Africa Week 2026 at its Paris Headquarters from May 19–22 under the theme "Ensuring Sustainable Availability of Water and Safe Sanitation Systems to Achieve the Objectives of Agenda 2063." A ministerial panel on water security opened the week, gathering representatives of Member States, the African Union, NGOs, and youth to address inequities in access to safe water, the impact of climate change on water resources, and the role of women and Indigenous knowledge in sustainable management. The week closed with an NGO Forum on women and Indigenous knowledge for peacebuilding and a special session on "Cultural Heritage, Water and Sanitation in Africa."
- Source: [Africa Week 2026: Ensuring Sustainable Availability of Water and Safe Sanitation Systems](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/africa-week-2026-ensuring-sustainable-availability-water-and-safe-sanitation-systems-achieve)

## Central Asian Countries Strengthen Cryosphere Cooperation at RES 2026

- Date: 2026-05-30
- Summary: The UNESCO Regional Office in Almaty organized the session "Cryosphere of Central Asia: from scientific assessment to joint climate adaptation action" during the Regional Ecological Summit (RES 2026) in Astana, Kazakhstan, in cooperation with the Kazakhstan Ministries of Ecology and Science. The session resulted in Central Asian countries adopting a joint statement of support for the Joint Subregional Action Programme (JSAP) on the cryosphere — a framework document developed with UNESCO support to strengthen regional cooperation in monitoring glaciers, snow cover, and permafrost, and to align climate adaptation approaches. Under a high-emissions scenario (SSP5-8.5), Central Asia could lose up to 85% of its glacier ice volume by 2100, with severe implications for water resources, infrastructure, and disaster risk.
- Source: [Central Asian countries strengthen cooperation on the cryosphere at RES 2026](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/central-asian-countries-strengthen-cooperation-cryosphere-res-2026)

## Regional Coordination Forum on Glacier and Cryosphere Monitoring in Central Asia

- Date: 2026-05-25
- Summary: On May 25, 2026, UNESCO co-organized the Central Asian Coordination Forum on Glacier and Cryosphere Monitoring in Tajikistan, alongside the Agency for Hydrometeorology of Tajikistan, GIZ, IOM, ADB, and the Aga Khan Foundation. The forum built on momentum from RES 2026 in Astana, advancing the Joint Subregional Action Programme (JSAP) on the cryosphere and reinforcing commitments to data exchange and joint adaptation measures across the region. This gathering confirmed the region's readiness to translate the political declaration from Astana into operational scientific cooperation.
- Source: [UNESCO supports regional dialogue on glacier and cryosphere monitoring in Central Asia](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-supports-regional-dialogue-glacier-and-cryosphere-monitoring-central-asia)

## Ocean Acidification: A Growing Threat to Marine Life (IOC UNESCO)

- Date: 2026-05-15
- Summary: UNESCO's Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission (IOC) published a report warning that the ocean absorbs about 25% of human-made CO₂ annually, causing increasing acidification that makes it difficult for corals, molluscs, and other marine organisms to form shells and skeletons, severely disrupting marine ecosystems and biodiversity. Ocean acidification is now recognized as a major driver of biodiversity loss with far-reaching consequences for both marine ecosystems and the coastal and fishing communities dependent on them. The IOC called for strengthened international cooperation in monitoring, early warning, and mitigation of ocean acidification impacts alongside broader climate action.
- Source: [Ocean acidification: a threat to marine life – IOC UNESCO](https://www.ioc.unesco.org/en/articles/ocean-acidification-threat-marine-life)
