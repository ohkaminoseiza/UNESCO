# UNESCO Environment Weekly Report 2026-07-11

## Overview

The week of July 4–11, 2026 was an active one for UNESCO's environmental agenda, spanning marine ecosystems, climate risk, biodiversity, and green education. The Large Marine Ecosystems Consultation Meeting (LME25) took place July 7–9 at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris, convening the global marine conservation community for strategic discussions. Preparations intensified for the first Global Conference of the International Decade of Sciences for Sustainable Development (IDSSD), scheduled for July 15–17 at UNESCO HQ. UNESCO also called for urgent action to accelerate green schools ahead of COP30, citing new data showing 96,000 schools in 93 countries have adopted green practices. Additional notable developments included the elevation of UNESCO's Climate Risk Informed Decision Analysis (CRIDA) methodology to flagship initiative status, and new content on biodiversity restoration and urban wildlife from UNESCO's Courier magazine.

---

## Large Marine Ecosystems Consultation Meeting (LME25)

**Date:** July 7–9, 2026  
**Source:** https://www.ioc.unesco.org/en/lme-learn

The 25th Annual Consultation Meeting on Large Marine Ecosystems (LME25) was held at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris over three days, organized by UNESCO's Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission (IOC). The meeting serves as a premier interactive platform for sharing expertise and conducting strategic discussions, engaging primarily the Global Environment Facility (GEF)-funded marine portfolio alongside a wide range of stakeholders, including representatives from Regional Seas bodies, Fisheries Management Organizations, international NGOs, and partner organizations. The annual consultation fosters and enhances cooperation within the LME community, working toward sustainable management of marine ecosystems and the conservation of coastal and ocean biodiversity.

## 2026 Global Conference of the International Decade of Sciences for Sustainable Development (July 15–17)

**Date:** July 2026 (conference upcoming; article published this week)  
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/2026-global-conference-international-decade-sciences-sustainable-development-2024-2033

UNESCO is hosting the first Global Conference of the International Decade of Sciences for Sustainable Development (IDSSD, 2024–2033) from July 15–17, 2026, at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris. Under the theme "Science in action: Charting a sustainable and equitable future for all," the event will demonstrate how transdisciplinary research, open science, and strengthened science–policy–society interfaces can drive more inclusive and equitable futures. Parallel thematic sessions will address Climate Change and Resilience, Biodiversity and Nature-based Solutions, Water Security for All, and Harnessing AI for Sustainable Development. The conference will also spotlight how science diplomacy supports international cooperation on global commons—including oceans, climate, biodiversity, glaciers, and emerging technologies—as a vehicle for collective progress toward the Sustainable Development Goals.

## COP30: UNESCO Calls for Urgent Action to Accelerate Green Schools

**Date:** July 2026  
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/cop30-unesco-calls-urgent-action-accelerate-green-schools

Ahead of COP30, UNESCO published new data showing that 96,000 schools across 93 countries have adopted green practices recommended by the Organization. UNESCO is calling on governments and the education sector to continue integrating environmental issues into all school curricula and to work toward the target of 50% of schools in each country being green by 2030. As part of the Greening Education Partnership (GEP), UNESCO plans to unveil new tools at COP30, including the GEP Dashboard and sustainable school building guidance, aimed at providing policymakers, school leaders, teachers, and other stakeholders with concrete principles for greening school buildings. In 2026, the Brazilian Ministry of Education and UNESCO will launch a free "Greening digital education" course for basic education teachers throughout Brazil, available via the AVAMEC platform.

## UNESCO's Climate Risk Analysis Method "CRIDA" Elevated to Flagship Initiative Status

**Date:** July 2026 (IHP endorsement: June 7, 2026)  
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-method-analysing-climate-risk-gets-flagship-initiative-status

UNESCO's Intergovernmental Hydrological Programme (IHP) endorsed the elevation of Climate Risk Informed Decision Analysis (CRIDA) to flagship initiative status, granting it increased visibility, resources, and institutional support. CRIDA is a UNESCO-developed methodology that provides governments and organizations with a practical framework for adaptive, resilient decision-making in the face of climate uncertainty. By becoming a flagship initiative, CRIDA is positioned to significantly scale its global application, helping more countries and communities integrate evidence-based climate risk analysis into water management, infrastructure planning, and climate adaptation strategies.

## Restoring Biodiversity, Reviving Life

**Date:** July 2026  
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/restoring-biodiversity-reviving-life

UNESCO published new content highlighting the critical importance of biodiversity restoration and its deep interconnection with climate change. The article underscores that biodiversity loss and climate change are mutually reinforcing crises: climate change drives biodiversity erosion, while degraded ecosystems lose their capacity to absorb and store CO2, thereby accelerating climate change. UNESCO's World Heritage sites are held up as models demonstrating that safeguarding cultural and natural heritage can simultaneously strengthen climate resilience, recover biodiversity, and sustain local livelihoods. The article calls for urgent, integrated approaches to conservation that recognize this interdependence.

## Wildlife: Thriving in the Concrete Jungle (UNESCO Courier)

**Date:** July 2026  
**Source:** https://courier.unesco.org/en/articles/wildlife-thriving-concrete-jungle

UNESCO's Courier magazine published a feature on urban wildlife, exploring how many species are adapting with surprising resilience to life in rapidly expanding cities. The article highlights the importance of urban green spaces, ecological corridors, and ecosystem services in supporting biodiversity within urban environments. It examines the potential for sustainable urban design to coexist with wildlife, making a case for integrating biodiversity considerations into city planning as a key component of sustainable development and human well-being.
