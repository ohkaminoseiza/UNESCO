# UNESCO AI Weekly Report 2026-10-31

## Overview

During the week of October 24–31, 2026, UNESCO's AI-related activities focused on AI in the judiciary and AI literacy education in Latin America and the Caribbean. An exploratory dialogue was held with Chile's Supreme Court to explore AI cooperation with the judiciary, while UNESCO Santiago marked the 25th anniversary of the Information for All Programme (IFAP) with two regional events on critical AI literacy in education.

## The Supreme Court of Chile and UNESCO Strengthen Dialogue on Artificial Intelligence

**Date:** 30 October 2026
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/supreme-court-chile-and-unesco-strengthen-dialogue-artificial-intelligence

On 26 October 2026, Esther Kuisch Laroche, Director of UNESCO's Regional Office in Santiago, and Guilherme Canela, Director of UNESCO's Division for Digital Inclusion, Policies and Digital Transformation in Paris, met with Gloria Ana Chevesich, President of Chile's Supreme Court. The meeting focused on the growing role of artificial intelligence in the judicial sphere.

UNESCO presented its Global Initiative for Judges, which since 2013 has supported more than 39,000 judicial actors in over 160 countries. The cooperation between UNESCO and Chile's judiciary dates to 2014, when Chile hosted the 17th Ibero-American Judicial Summit. While no formal agreement was announced, both institutions expressed interest in future collaboration on AI training and knowledge exchange. Kuisch Laroche stated: "We greatly value this opportunity for dialogue with the Supreme Court of Chile."

## UNESCO Santiago Marks 25th Anniversary of the Information for All Programme with Regional AI and Critical Literacy Events

**Date:** 29 October 2026
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-santiago-commemorated-25th-anniversary-information-all-programme-ifap-regional-events

UNESCO's Regional Office in Santiago celebrated the 25th anniversary of IFAP with two regional events: one in Montevideo (15 October) and one in Santiago (26 October). Both events were part of an IFAP-funded initiative run through UNESCO's Observatory on Artificial Intelligence in Education for Latin America and the Caribbean, and one of three projects the programme supported worldwide in 2026.

In Montevideo, held at the MERCOSUR Building with Uruguay's Ministry of Education and Culture as part of EduIA Conf 2026, speakers from Chile, Argentina, and Uruguay argued that AI literacy must go beyond tool use to encompass information evaluation, detection of bias and misinformation, and understanding of ethical and privacy issues. In Santiago, co-organized with Universidad Alberto Hurtado during AI Week and Global Media and Information Literacy Week, Guilherme Canela delivered a keynote on information integrity as a public good, with discussions focusing on misinformation and civic capacity-building.
