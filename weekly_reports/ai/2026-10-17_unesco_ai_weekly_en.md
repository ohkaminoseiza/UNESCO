# UNESCO AI Weekly Report 2026-10-17

## Overview

During the period of 10–17 October 2026, UNESCO published several updates on AI ethics, education, and application. On 14 October, UNESCO released Bangladesh's AI Readiness Assessment Report, outlining 15 recommendations for responsible AI governance. On 15 October, UNESCO and CODEMAO reported on Africa-Asia cooperation in AI education through the Youth AI Tour. On 16 October, an article profiled how a content creator in India is using AI and digital channels to expand access to sexuality education. The third cohort of UNESCO's free digital citizenship and AI MOOC also started on 12 October, now available in Portuguese for the first time.

---

## Bangladesh: AI Readiness Assessment Report

**Date:** 14 October 2026
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/bangladesh-artificial-intelligence-readiness-assessment-report

UNESCO, Bangladesh's ICT Division, a2i, and UNDP jointly published Bangladesh's AI Readiness Assessment Report on 14 October 2026. Using UNESCO's Readiness Assessment Methodology (RAM), the report evaluates Bangladesh's preparedness to implement UNESCO's 2021 Recommendation on the Ethics of AI across five dimensions: legal and regulatory, social and cultural, scientific and educational, economic, and technical and infrastructural. Based on desk research and consultations with government, academia, civil society, and the private sector, it identifies priorities for AI governance, digital divides, skills, and data and infrastructure access, and presents 15 actionable recommendations. The work was carried out with financial support from the European Union.

## UNESCO and CODEMAO Advance Africa-Asia Cooperation in AI Education through Youth AI Tour

**Date:** 15 October 2026
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-and-codemao-advance-africa-asia-cooperation-ai-education-through-youth-ai-tour

UNESCO and CODEMAO, a Chinese education technology company, reported progress in Africa-Asia cooperation in AI education through the Youth AI Tour. The initiative aims to equip young people with a deeper understanding of AI and the capacity to use it ethically and creatively, emphasizing the importance of cross-regional cooperation. Further details on tour locations and participation numbers are available on the official UNESCO page (article body partially confirmed).

## How Karishma from India Broadens Access to Sexuality Education via AI and Digital Channels

**Date:** 16 October 2026
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/how-karishma-india-broadens-access-sexuality-education-ai-and-digital-channels

The article profiles Karishma Swarup, a 29-year-old content creator from Kolkata, India, who uses digital platforms to deliver comprehensive sexuality education (CSE) to young people. With UNESCO's support, she and Artika Singh launched an online CSE course, reaching tens of thousands of young people through social media. The piece also addresses the risks of AI tools — including inaccurate information, bias, and emotional dependency — and calls for teaching young people to critically evaluate AI-generated content. Karishma also outlines the role she sees for UNESCO in setting standards and connecting educators with technology companies.

## UNESCO Opens Third Edition of Digital Citizenship and AI MOOC, Now Also in Portuguese

**Date (course start):** 12 October 2026 (announcement article: 25 September 2026)
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-opens-third-edition-its-course-digital-citizenship-and-artificial-intelligence-now-also

UNESCO's Regional Office in Montevideo, in partnership with Microsoft, launched the third cohort of its free online course "Educating in the Age of Artificial Intelligence: Digital Citizenship at School" on 12 October 2026. The course is now available in Portuguese for the first time, joining the existing English and Spanish versions. Designed primarily for teachers, education teams, and social actors, it consists of five modules covering digital footprints, disinformation, the ethical use of AI, and democratic participation. Participants who complete at least 80% of the course content and assessments are eligible for an official UNESCO certificate. The first two cohorts attracted over 33,000 registrations from more than 113 countries.
