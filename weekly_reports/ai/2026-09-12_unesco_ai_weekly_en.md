# UNESCO AI Weekly Report 2026-09-12

## Overview

During the week of September 5–12, 2026, UNESCO carried out significant activities related to artificial intelligence governance and ethics. The Digital Learning Week 2026 (September 8–11) at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris brought together over 25 education ministers who adopted a joint ministerial statement affirming that education must remain "a human right and a common good in the age of AI." On September 9, UNESCO unveiled three new AI governance tools in preparation for the 4th Global Forum on the Ethics of AI (September 14–17, Riyadh). A major UNESCO IESALC study on AI adoption in higher education across Latin America and the Caribbean was also published, revealing a significant governance gap despite widespread AI use. These developments underscore UNESCO's intensified global leadership in shaping ethical and inclusive AI governance frameworks.

---

## UNESCO Launches New Tools to Reinforce Ethical AI Governance at Global Forum in Riyadh

**Date:** September 9, 2026

**Summary:**
UNESCO introduced three new tools at the 4th Global Forum on the Ethics of AI in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, designed to help policymakers develop ethical and inclusive artificial intelligence policies. The resources include an updated Readiness Assessment Methodology (RAM), a global AI governance trend analysis instrument, and a new Environmental Toolkit. The forum, held under the theme "Transforming Global Cooperation for Ethical AI Governance," is expected to convene approximately 8,000 participants from around 50 countries from September 14–17, 2026.

## New UNESCO IESALC Study Reveals Widespread AI Adoption in Higher Education Across Latin America and the Caribbean, While Governance Lags Behind

**Date:** September 9, 2026

**Summary:**
A UNESCO IESALC study of over 200 higher education institutions across 19 Latin American and Caribbean nations found that 87% are using AI in at least one area of their activities, with teaching and learning being the most common application. Despite this high adoption rate, only 26% of institutions have a formal AI strategy in place, revealing a critical governance deficit. The report warns that institutions are adopting AI faster than they can establish appropriate oversight frameworks and policies.

## Education Ministers Call for Education to Remain a Common Good in the Age of AI at UNESCO's Digital Learning Week

**Date:** September 9, 2026

**Summary:**
Over 25 education ministers at UNESCO's Digital Learning Week 2026 adopted a joint ministerial statement declaring that education must be "sustained as a human right and a common good in the age of artificial intelligence." The statement outlines eight priorities, including protecting teacher agency, safeguarding learners through age-appropriate AI measures, ensuring public oversight of AI systems in education, and building institutional governance capacity. A UNESCO survey released alongside the statement found that 93% of academics believe students use generative AI for assignments, and two-thirds believe professional guidelines on the issue would be helpful.

**Source:** [https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/education-ministers-call-education-remain-common-good-age-ai-unescos-digital-learning-week](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/education-ministers-call-education-remain-common-good-age-ai-unescos-digital-learning-week)

## Digital Learning Week 2026: "Education in the Age of AI: Facts | Frictions | Frontiers"

**Date:** September 8–11, 2026

**Summary:**
UNESCO's Digital Learning Week 2026 convened over 1,000 participants — including ministers, technology industry leaders, educators, and students — at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris under the theme "Education in the age of AI: Facts | Frictions | Frontiers." The event examined what counts as fact in an era of synthetic knowledge, how to navigate the frictions AI is generating across education systems, and what new frontiers AI is opening up for learning. The 2026 UNESCO ICT in Education Prize award ceremony took place on September 9.

**Source:** [https://www.unesco.org/en/weeks/digital-learning](https://www.unesco.org/en/weeks/digital-learning)

## UNESCO ICT in Education Prize 2026 Awarded to Innovative Projects from Finland and Brazil

**Date:** September 9, 2026

**Summary:**
UNESCO awarded the 2026 King Hamad Bin Isa Al-Khalifa Prize for the Use of ICT in Education to outstanding projects from Finland and Brazil. Both projects were recognized for their use of information and communication technology to foster creative problem-solving and critical thinking skills among learners. The award ceremony was held as part of Digital Learning Week at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris.

**Source:** [https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-ict-prize-awards-dynamic-projects-promoting-creativity-and-critical-thinking-finland-and](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-ict-prize-awards-dynamic-projects-promoting-creativity-and-critical-thinking-finland-and)
