# UNESCO AI Weekly Report 2026-11-14

## Overview

During the week of 7–14 November 2026, UNESCO announced its participation in an EU-funded research project to promote responsible AI in the cultural and creative sectors, and released a new Media and Information Literacy curriculum guide for educators and learners during Global MIL Week 2026.

## UNESCO Joins EU-Funded CULTURAI Project to Advance Responsible AI in the Cultural and Creative Sectors

**Date:** 10 November 2026  
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-joins-eu-funded-culturai-project-advance-responsible-ai-cultural-and-creative-sectors

UNESCO has joined CULTURAI, a four-year research project funded by the EU's Horizon Europe programme, to study how AI can be used responsibly in audiovisual and public cultural institutions while protecting creativity and diversity. UNESCO is leading a comparative review of cultural-sector AI governance in seven jurisdictions and will survey approximately 800 audiovisual professionals about how AI affects their creative autonomy and working conditions. The goal is to translate ethical AI commitments into practical tools and policy recommendations for the cultural sector.

## AI Answers. You Decide: UNESCO Launches a New Media and Information Literacy Curriculum

**Date:** 10 November 2026  
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-answers-you-decide-unesco-launches-new-media-and-information-literacy-curriculum

UNESCO released a companion guide during Global Media and Information Literacy Week 2026 to help educators and learners think critically about AI's expanding role in information. Building on its earlier MIL curriculum, the guide includes discussion questions, activities, and examples across five themes—from everyday AI use to human rights and the future of AI. It focuses on how AI shapes knowledge, society, and power rather than on specific tools.
